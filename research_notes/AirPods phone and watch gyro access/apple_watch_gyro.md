# Apple Watch gyroscope and motion data: reading it on watchOS and getting it to a computer (as of 2026-11-07)

Source dating: unless noted, Apple documentation was read on 2026-11-07 from Apple's documentation JSON (same text as the linked developer.apple.com pages). App Store listings were read on 2026-11-07. Forum posts, papers and articles carry their own dates in the bullets.

## Q1. Which motion data can a watchOS app read, at what maximum rates, on which models and watchOS versions, and under what conditions?

### Takeaway
A watchOS app gets gyroscope-derived data through Core Motion device motion (`CMDeviceMotion`: attitude quaternion, bias-corrected `rotationRate`, gravity, `userAcceleration`). With `CMMotionManager` that is live, per sample, up to 100 Hz; with `CMBatchedSensorManager` (watchOS 10+) it is 200 Hz device motion and 800 Hz accelerometer, delivered one batch per second and only during an active HealthKit workout session (Apple named Series 8 and Ultra in 2023). Raw gyroscope (`CMGyroData` / `startGyroUpdates`) has been reported unavailable on Apple Watch (`isGyroAvailable` false) on Series 3, 7 and 8, so use `deviceMotion.rotationRate`. To keep sensors running with the wrist down, run an `HKWorkoutSession` (or a time-limited extended runtime session); `CMSensorRecorder` can log accelerometer only (50 Hz) in the background for later pickup.

### Cited Findings
- CMMotionManager (watchOS 2.0+) offers accelerometer, gyroscope, magnetometer and sensor-fused device motion ("attitude, rotation rate, calibrated magnetic fields, the direction of gravity, and the amount of acceleration the user contributes"); "Create only one CMMotionManager object for your app. Multiple instances of this class can affect the rate at which the system receives data" — [Apple: CMMotionManager](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/coremotion/cmmotionmanager)
- "If a hardware feature (for example, a gyroscope) is not available on a device, calling a start method related to that feature has no effect"; check `isGyroAvailable` — [Apple: CMMotionManager](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/coremotion/cmmotionmanager); if it is false, "starting updates doesn't deliver any data to your app" — [Apple: Getting raw gyroscope events](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/coremotion/getting-raw-gyroscope-events)
- Raw gyro values "may be biased by other factors such as temperature. If your app requires unbiased rotation values, use the device-motion interfaces instead"; maximum raw gyro rate "is hardware-dependent but is usually at least 100 Hz", and over-asking gets the hardware maximum — [Apple: Getting raw gyroscope events](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/coremotion/getting-raw-gyroscope-events)
- `deviceMotionUpdateInterval` is capped by hardware; Apple says to "check the timestamps of the delivered CMDeviceMotion instances to determine the true update interval" — [Apple: deviceMotionUpdateInterval](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/coremotion/cmmotionmanager/devicemotionupdateinterval)
- Raw gyro unavailable on Apple Watch, user reports: Series 3 / v5.2.1 (Nov 2019), with an Apple engineer reply (Apr 2021): "If it is returning 'false', that means gyroscope data is not available to be used. There is no solution or a workaround to bypass or ignore this check." — [Apple Developer Forums 126215](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/126215); Series 7 (Jul 2022: accelerometer works, gyroscope does not; unanswered) — [Forums 710444](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/710444); "Apple Watch 8, WatchOS 9.1" (Nov 2022) `isGyroAvailable` always false; Oct 2023 follow-up says gyroscope and magnetometer report false — [Forums 719594](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/719594)
- Practical CMMotionManager rate (Aug 2025): a sports-app developer reports "With Core Motion, I can currently get deviceMotion updates at ~100 Hz" and asks for 500-1000 Hz; the only Apple reply (Frameworks Engineer, Aug 2025) points to WWDC23's "High-Frequency Motion API for Apple Watch" — [Forums 796412](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/796412)
- WWDC23 session 10179 (June 2023; transcript read 2026-11-07): CMMotionManager "maximum supported frequency is 100 Hz", "a great choice if you have low latency requirements on a sub-second scale, or have motion-based features outside of workouts"; CMBatchedSensorManager "provides batches of sensor data on a fixed schedule, delivering a batch of data per second"; "That's 800 Hz accelerometer and 200 Hz device motion, compared to 100 Hz with the existing CMMotionManager"; suited to "workout-centric features that can benefit from high rate data, but without very tight latency requirements"; "you need to have an active HealthKit workout session to get data"; "Apple Watch Series 8 and Ultra support both high rate accelerometer and device motion"; "errors are surfaced if we aren't authorized for motion data or on an unsupported platform"; the transcript never mentions iPhone support — [WWDC23 "What's new in Core Motion"](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2023/10179/)
- CMBatchedSensorManager is watchOS 10.0+; class-level `isAccelerometerSupported` / `isDeviceMotionSupported`, read-only `accelerometerDataFrequency` / `deviceMotionDataFrequency`, async sequences `accelerometerUpdates()` / `deviceMotionUpdates()` — [Apple: CMBatchedSensorManager](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/coremotion/cmbatchedsensormanager); the iOS 27.2 SDK header declares `API_AVAILABLE(watchos(10.0)) API_UNAVAILABLE(tvos) API_UNAVAILABLE(macos)`, and `accelerometerSupported` "Reflects whether batched accelerometer data is supported on this platform" — [CMBatchedSensorManager.h, third-party mirror of Apple's iPhoneOS 27.2 SDK](https://github.com/xybp888/iOS-SDKs/blob/master/iPhoneOS27.2.sdk/System/Library/Frameworks/CoreMotion.framework/Headers/CMBatchedSensorManager.h)
- Apple's Core Motion change log: June 2023 CMBatchedSensorManager "to access batches of high-frequency accelerometer and device motion data during workouts, such as a golf swing or a baseball bat swing"; June 2024 CMHeadphoneMotionManager can supply AirPods device motion on watchOS; Sept 2024 shallow depth/pressure on Series 10; no entries after September 2024 — [Apple: Core Motion updates](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/updates/coremotion)
- New in the 2026 SDKs: `CMMotionManager.deviceMotionBody` (iOS/watchOS 27.0) re-expresses "3D orientation, rotation rate, gravity, and user acceleration" relative to a chosen view/body; "On platforms where body-relative motion calculation isn't supported, Core Motion reports motion data using the default device coordinate frame" — [Apple: deviceMotionBody](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/coremotion/cmmotionmanager/devicemotionbody)
- CMSensorRecorder (watchOS 2.0+): `recordAccelerometer(forDuration:)` captures accelerometer only "at a sample rate of 50 Hz ... even if your app is suspended or terminated. The collected data remains accessible to your app for up to three days" — [Apple: recordAccelerometer(forDuration:)](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/coremotion/cmsensorrecorder/recordaccelerometer(forduration:)); needs `NSMotionUsageDescription` — [Apple: CMSensorRecorder](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/coremotion/cmsensorrecorder)
- Shipping-app rates: SensorLog on Apple Watch logs all selected sensors "in the background with up to 50Hz", individual sensors "up to 100Hz" (sessions up to 1 h); for longer sessions "In the background only logging of the accelerometer data with max. 50Hz is supported. In the foreground all sensor data with up to 100Hz" — [SensorLog App Store listing (UK)](https://apps.apple.com/gb/app/sensorlog/id388014573); Care Sensors records "Device Motion (attitude, quaternion, rotation rate, user acceleration, gravity) and Raw Accelerometer simultaneously at 5–100 Hz" on Series 4 or later, watchOS 10.0+ — [Care Sensors App Store listing](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/care-sensors/id6720754155)
- Devices and OS as of Nov 2026: "watchOS 27 is compatible with" Apple Watch SE 3, Series 9 or newer, Ultra 2 or newer; "Updating to watchOS 27 requires iPhone 11 or later, or iPhone SE (2nd generation) or later, with iOS 27" (page published 2026-09-14) — [Apple Support: Update your Apple Watch](https://support.apple.com/en-us/108926); Series 8, Ultra 1 and older SE models are not supported — [iClarified, 2026-06-09](https://www.iclarified.com/101134/watchos-27-supported-devices-full-list-of-compatible-apple-watches); Apple's 2026-09-09 press release introduced Apple Watch Ultra 4 (and references Apple Watch Series 12), "available to pre-order today, with availability beginning Friday, September 18", with watchOS 27 arriving Monday, September 14; the release does not mention the accelerometer, gyroscope or motion sampling rates — [Apple Newsroom: Apple unveils Apple Watch Ultra 4](https://www.apple.com/newsroom/2026/09/apple-unveils-apple-watch-ultra-4/); [Apple Newsroom: lineups in stores](https://www.apple.com/newsroom/2026/09/the-latest-iphone-apple-watch-and-airpods-lineups-arrive-in-stores-worldwide/); a Japanese report gives 2026-09-15 (Japan time) for watchOS 27 — [mobareco.jp](https://mobareco.jp/ca-news/a361054/)

#### Keeping sensors running
- An Apple Watch app "normally transitions to the background and becomes suspended when the user lowers their wrist"; background sessions "can only monitor workouts, track the user's location, or play audio files" — [Apple: Using extended runtime sessions](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/watchkit/using-extended-runtime-sessions)
- Workout sessions need the "Workout processing" background mode; with an active session the app "continues to run throughout the entire workout session, even when the user lowers their wrist or interacts with a different app" and "continues to receive data from HealthKit and Apple Watch's sensors in the background"; "If your app uses an excessive amount of CPU while in the background, watchOS may suspend it"; the app must clearly show a workout is in progress and save or discard it — [Apple: Running workout sessions](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/healthkit/running-workout-sessions)
- Apple's SwingWatch sample (updated 2016-09-13, watchOS 3): "The HealthKit workout session enables background processing of the Device Motion samples." — [Apple sample code: SwingWatch README](https://developer.apple.com/library/archive/samplecode/SwingWatch/Listings/README_md.html)
- Extended runtime sessions (WKExtendedRuntimeSession, watchOS 6.0+), one type per app: Self care (frontmost, 10 min), Mindfulness (frontmost, 1 hour), Physical therapy (background, 1 hour), Smart alarm (background, schedulable within 36 h, 30 min); must start while the app is active; sustained high CPU can cancel the session (`exceededResourceLimits`); Apple's example: a physical therapy app uses WKExtendedRuntimeSession "for range-of-motion exercises" so they don't affect Move/Exercise rings — [Apple: Using extended runtime sessions](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/watchkit/using-extended-runtime-sessions); [Apple: WKExtendedRuntimeSession](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/watchkit/wkextendedruntimesession)
- Shipping apps rely on workout sessions: TDInput "uses a HealthKit workout session to keep sensors active"; "Watch battery lasts approximately 4-5 hours of continuous streaming" — [TDInput App Store listing](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/tdinput/id6760799367); Sensor Logger: "For Apple Watch users, you have to enable the Heart Rate sensor in order to collect measurements in the background" — [Sensor Logger help](https://www.tszheichoi.com/sensorloggerhelp); a 2023 tutorial's code comment says "sensor data can be collected only during workout" and starts an HKWorkoutSession before CMBatchedSensorManager — [Hackernoon, Pavel Shadrin, 2023-10-09](https://hackernoon.com/getting-hiqh-frequency-tennis-motion-data-from-apple-watch)

### Inferences
- "Gyroscope" on the watch in practice means `CMDeviceMotion.rotationRate` (rad/s, bias-corrected) plus attitude; do not design around `startGyroUpdates`.
- For live use, CMMotionManager device motion at 50-100 Hz inside an HKWorkoutSession is the standard pattern (TDInput, Sensor Logger, SensorLog, SwingWatch). CMBatchedSensorManager adds at least about 1 s of delay by design, so it suits recording and offline analysis of swings/impacts.
- Every watchOS 27 device is newer than the Series 8 / Ultra hardware Apple named for CMBatchedSensorManager, so support on Series 9+ and Ultra 2+ is likely but not confirmed by Apple; check `isDeviceMotionSupported` at runtime. Series 8 and Ultra 1 cannot run watchOS 27.

### Gaps
- No Apple list of models supporting CMBatchedSensorManager beyond "Series 8 and Ultra" (2023); SE 3, Series 12 and Ultra 4 support is undocumented.
- Apple does not document whether CMMotionManager's 100 Hz cap holds in the background during a workout; SensorLog's 50 Hz background multi-sensor limit suggests throttling in practice, but that is one developer's claim.

## Q2. Fastest practical path from watch to laptop; what TN3135 allows and forbids; reported latency and throughput

### Takeaway
watchOS blocks low-level networking (Network framework `NWConnection`, UDP/TCP, `URLSessionWebSocketTask`, `URLSessionStreamTask`, Bonjour; BSD sockets never work) for ordinary apps; only HTTP/HTTPS through URLSession is open to all apps, and Apple DTS says even that does not reach a LAN IP by default. The practical live path is watch -> iPhone over WatchConnectivity (`sendMessage` / `sendMessageData`, batched, while a workout session keeps the watch app reachable) -> laptop over Wi-Fi from the iPhone app (UDP/OSC, WebSocket or HTTP, which iOS allows with Local Network permission). Watch-only HTTPS to an internet-reachable server also works but is slow (SensorLog caps it at 10 Hz). No one publishes measured modern WatchConnectivity latency; research pipelines that relay watch IMU via the iPhone run at 25-30 Hz.

### Cited Findings
- TN3135 definitions: high-level networking = "the HTTP and HTTPS support in URLSession, and any code layered on top of that"; low-level = "Network framework, Stream, and any other API that runs a TCP connection or UDP session directly. That includes the low-level aspects of URLSession, namely URLSessionStreamTask and URLSessionWebSocketTask", plus Bonjour APIs (NWBrowser, NetService) — [Apple TN3135](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/technotes/tn3135-low-level-networking-on-watchos)
- TN3135 allowed cases only: audio streaming apps "while actively streaming audio" (watchOS 6+); VoIP apps during a CallKit call (watchOS 9+); an application service listener for the same app on tvOS via DeviceDiscoveryUI (watchOS 9 / tvOS 16). Otherwise "if a normal app attempts to start an NWConnection, that connection will stay in the NWConnection.State.waiting(_:) state with an error of ENETDOWN" and NWPathMonitor stays unsatisfied — [Apple TN3135](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/technotes/tn3135-low-level-networking-on-watchos)
- TN3135: watchOS 6-8 had a bug letting low-level networking work, "fixed in watchOS 9"; "The BSD sockets API doesn't work for networking on watchOS under any circumstances"; "the simulator always allows low-level networking", so test on a device, with and without the paired iPhone; revision history: 2021-05-14 added URLSessionStreamTask/URLSessionWebSocketTask as low-level; latest revision 2026-07-16 ("Fixed a broken link"), so the rules are unchanged in 2026 — [Apple TN3135](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/technotes/tn3135-low-level-networking-on-watchos)
- Apple DTS (Quinn "The Eskimo!", May 2025, accepted answer) on a watchOS URLSession request to `http://10.15.48.191:9000/hello` failing with -1001: "This won't work by default. While you're using a high-level API, URLSession, getting this to work would require low-level network access, which isn't the standard on watchOS." — [Apple Developer Forums 785846](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/785846)
- SensorLog history shows the rule hitting a real app: Dec 2019 the watch app gained "stream sensor data via tcp/ip ... (requires connected iPhone with LAN/WAN connection)"; v5.3 (March 2023): "Unfortunately had to remove tcp/ip streaming and Remote Sensorlog feature, due to changes in the low level networking functions of watchOS"; v6.0 (Nov 2024): "standalone version of watch App", "upload of log files via HTTPS"; the watch can still send to a REST API "with up to 10Hz" — [SensorLog App Store listing (UK)](https://apps.apple.com/gb/app/sensorlog/id388014573)
- WatchConnectivity: `sendMessage` "Sends a message immediately to the paired and active device"; messages "are queued serially and delivered in the order in which you sent them"; calling it from the active watch app "wakes up the corresponding iOS app in the background and makes it reachable" (the reverse does not wake the watch app) — [Apple: sendMessage(_:replyHandler:errorHandler:)](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/watchconnectivity/wcsession/sendmessage(_:replyhandler:errorhandler:)); `sendMessageData` behaves the same for a `Data` payload — [Apple: sendMessageData](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/watchconnectivity/wcsession/sendmessagedata(_:replyhandler:errorhandler:))
- `isReachable` is true on the watch side when the iPhone is in range and the watch app "is running in the foreground, or is running with a high priority in the background (for example, during a workout session...)"; sending to an unreachable counterpart "results in an error" — [Apple: WCSession.isReachable](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/watchconnectivity/wcsession/isreachable)
- `WCError.Code.payloadTooLarge` exists "for both data dictionaries and files", but Apple gives no byte limit — [Apple: payloadTooLarge](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/watchconnectivity/wcerror/code/payloadtoolarge); "Most transfers happen in the background when the receiving app is inactive ... Live communication is also possible when both apps are active." — [Apple: Watch Connectivity](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/watchconnectivity)
- Latency reports (anecdotal; mostly old): July 2015, watchOS 2 beta: a burst of `sendMessageData` messages sent over ~430 ms (the poster: "sending 21 messages in 430ms") took over 4 s to finish arriving on the iPhone (the first arrived after ~0.2 s), and some arrived "repeated and coming in out of order"; an Apple engineer suggested "restricting the frequency in which you send the messages to a couple per second or building a method of 'back pressure' on top of send message with reply" — [Forums 9368](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/9368); Feb 2019 (Series 3, watchOS 5.1): background `transferUserInfo` arrived in 2-10 s; reply: "The Watch Connectivity frameworks were not designed to exchange real-time information" — [Forums 113537](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/113537); Oct 2021 anecdote: replies take "1-3 s in general" — [Forums 692393](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/692393)
- Research pipelines: IMUPoser (CHI '23) used Apple Watch Series 6, iPhone 11 Pro and AirPods Pro; "The Apple Watch and AirPods communicated over Bluetooth to the iPhones", and "the two iPhones relayed all IMU data to a laptop"; system rate 25 Hz (the AirPods' maximum); mean inference 26.8 ms; no transport latency reported — [IMUPoser, Mollyn et al., CHI 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.12518); ArmPoser (arXiv 2026-09-08, ACM SUI '26): "we stream IMU data from an Apple Watch SE to a MacBook Air M3, which runs inference at 30 Hz"; poses arrive "about 170 ms after the corresponding IMU sample", which the authors attribute to the model's 5 future frames; streaming path not specified — [ArmPoser, arXiv 2609.08806](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.08806)
- A 2023 tutorial sends each CMBatchedSensorManager chunk to the iPhone with `sendMessage` during an HKWorkoutSession, to avoid payloads "too large to fit into a message payload that can be sent quickly"; no latency numbers — [Hackernoon, 2023-10-09](https://hackernoon.com/getting-hiqh-frequency-tennis-motion-data-from-apple-watch)
- iPhone side: any iOS 14+ app making "direct unicast or multicast connections to local hosts" must include `NSLocalNetworkUsageDescription` (Local Network permission) — [Apple: NSLocalNetworkUsageDescription](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/bundleresources/information-property-list/nslocalnetworkusagedescription)
- Bluetooth LE: Core Bluetooth is on watchOS 4.0+ and the watch can act as a central (CBCentralManager), but "In watchOS, tvOS, and visionOS, you can't advertise services using a CBPeripheralManager object" — [Apple: Core Bluetooth](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/corebluetooth); [Apple: CBPeripheralManager](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/corebluetooth/cbperipheralmanager); extended runtime sessions let an app "continue to communicate with Bluetooth devices ... even after the watch's screen turns off" — [Apple: WKExtendedRuntimeSession](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/watchkit/wkextendedruntimesession)
- Caution on unverified designs: a 2026 GitHub project describes an Apple Watch Ultra 3 app streaming "CoreMotion @ 100Hz" as JSON over WebSocket straight to a Python server, but the repo holds only README.md and PROJECT.md (no watch code), and TN3135 classes `URLSessionWebSocketTask` as blocked low-level networking — [FelipeDrummond/wrist-teleop](https://github.com/FelipeDrummond/wrist-teleop)

### Inferences
- Don't plan on WebSocket, UDP or calling a LAN HTTP server from the watch; it can work in the simulator and then fail on the device.
- Best custom design: CMMotionManager at 50-100 Hz in an HKWorkoutSession; pack 5-20 samples per `sendMessageData` (a few to ~10 messages/s, with reply-based back-pressure); the iPhone app forwards each batch to the laptop over UDP/OSC or WebSocket. Expect tens to hundreds of ms of delay with occasional multi-second stalls; timestamp every sample on the watch and resample on the laptop.
- Watch-only fallback: HTTPS POST of batches to an internet-reachable URL (cloud server or a tunnel to the laptop); SensorLog's 10 Hz cap suggests batches, not per-sample requests.
- BLE from the watch (central) to a laptop acting as a GATT peripheral is possible in principle (Windows can host a GATT server), but I found no working example; treat it as experimental.

### Gaps
- No modern (2023-2026) measurement of WatchConnectivity latency or throughput; numbers are anecdotes from 2015-2021, and the research papers report model delay, not transport delay. A Medium article reportedly streaming watch IMU at 50 Hz with sub-5 ms clock sync could not be fetched (HTTP 403), so it is unverified.
- No documented size limit for `sendMessage` payloads (a Stack Overflow question guessed ~65-70 KB; unverified).

## Q3. Existing apps that stream or record Apple Watch gyroscope data

### Takeaway
No-code options exist. Sensor Logger (free, optional paid tiers) records watch "wrist motion" (rotation rate, gravity, acceleration, quaternion, plus Euler angles) and, in Phone & Watch mode, can stream through the phone by HTTP Push or MQTT JSON; v1.68 (Oct 2026) added "High frequency wrist motion data from Apple Watch". TDInput ($0.99) is the only app found that is built for live streaming of watch motion to a computer: OSC over Wi-Fi via the iPhone, wrist orientation at 50 Hz plus rotation rate, acceleration and gravity. SensorLog (paid) logs watch device motion to CSV/JSON and can POST from the watch at up to 10 Hz, but dropped TCP/UDP streaming in 2023. Care Sensors (free) records watch device motion and raw accelerometer at 5-100 Hz to CSV/JSON. Sensor-Stream (free) shows and records watch accelerometer/gyroscope with CSV export but names no network protocol.

### Cited Findings
- Sensor Logger (iPhone app with Apple Watch companion): "Stream data via HTTP & MQTT"; exports "Zipped CSV, JSON, Excel, KML & SQLite"; free with "Optional Paid Features (Plus, Pro & Ultimate)"; watch requires watchOS 9.4 or later; current version 1.68.1 ("5d ago" on 2026-11-07); v1.68 (Oct 7): "High frequency wrist motion data from Apple Watch"; v1.63 (Aug 13): "Euler angles are now included in the streaming mode from watch"; v1.61 (Jul 13): audio recording on watches; version dates show no year, presumably 2026 — [Sensor Logger App Store listing (US)](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/sensor-logger/id1531582925)
- Sensor Logger HTTP Push: POST `application/json` with `messageId`, `sessionId`, `deviceId`, `payload:[{name, time, ...}]` "once every configurable batch period"; messageId helps because messages "can be received out-of-order"; MQTT uses the same schema; watch record type `"wrist motion"` with rotationRateX/Y/Z, gravityX/Y/Z, accelerationX/Y/Z, quaternionW/X/Y/Z — [awesome-sensor-logger PUSHING.md](https://github.com/tszheichoi/awesome-sensor-logger/blob/main/PUSHING.md)
- Sensor Logger modes: "Phone & Watch Mode" (phone nearby) "Works with other Sensor Logger features on your phone, such as Studies, Streaming and Rule Engines"; "Watch Only Mode" makes standalone recordings, "Data is transferred to the phone afterwards", and does not work with Streaming; wrist motion "includes rotation and acceleration data, all combined into a single CSV"; "Watch Disconnected" usually means the watch app isn't in the foreground; the help page gives no Hz values for wrist motion — [Sensor Logger help](https://www.tszheichoi.com/sensorloggerhelp)
- TDInput: "Stream 30+ sensor channels over OSC"; watch channels "Wrist Orientation — pitch, roll, and yaw at 50Hz", gravity vector, "Rotation Rate — angular velocity on each axis", acceleration, heart rate "via workout session", Digital Crown; OSC addresses `/watch/motion/pitch,roll,yaw`, `/watch/accel/x,y,z`, `/watch/gravity/x,y,z`, `/watch/rotation/x,y,z`; setup "Open TDInput on your iPhone and enter your Mac's IP address", OSC In CHOP on port 9000, "Standard OSC over your local Wi-Fi"; $0.99; iOS 18.6+ / watchOS 11.6+ — [TDInput App Store listing](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/tdinput/id6760799367)
- SensorLog: £2.99 (UK) plus optional tip purchases; sensors include CMAccelerometer, CMGyroData, CMMagnetometer, CMDeviceMotion (iPhone list); watch: background logging up to 50 Hz, individual sensors up to 100 Hz, HTTP(S) to a REST API "with up to 10Hz in JSON format (POST) or form-url encoded"; files in CSV or JSON; "Long Term Accelerometer Data ... for up to 3 days at 50Hz"; latest version 6.1 dated 05/11/2024 (5 Nov 2024), whose notes now read "This app has been updated by Apple to prepare for watchOS 27 compatibility"; requires watchOS 11.0+ — [SensorLog App Store listing (UK)](https://apps.apple.com/gb/app/sensorlog/id388014573)
- Care Sensors: free; "Collect Apple Watch motion data for any research purpose"; records device motion and raw accelerometer "simultaneously at 5–100 Hz" with four CoreMotion reference frames; live visualisation "on both the Watch (5–30 Hz) and the iPhone"; files saved on the watch, sent to the iPhone, CSV or JSON, ZIP export; Series 4 or later, watchOS 10.0+ — [Care Sensors App Store listing](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/care-sensors/id6720754155)
- Sensor-Stream: free; "Stream, visualize, and record real-time motion sensor data from your iPhone or Apple Watch with smooth live graphs and exportable CSV recordings"; "Accelerometer and gyroscope data", "Pitch, roll, yaw, quaternions, and rotation matrices"; watchOS 9.0+; names no network protocol — [Sensor-Stream App Store listing](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/sensor-stream/id6783206955)
- Watch Sensor Logger: free; records "acceleration, rotation rate, gravity, attitude" with CSV export; watchOS 8.5+; version history dates 2022-07-15 and 2023-11-30 (no later updates); a 2022 review says "Export doesn't work" — [Watch Sensor Logger App Store listing](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/watch-sensor-logger/id1634449655)
- GyroOSC streams roll/pitch/yaw over OSC "over UDP or TCP" but is "Only for iPhone" (no watch app); 10 minutes free, $4.99 unlock — [GyroOSC App Store listing](https://apps.apple.com/ca/app/gyroosc/id6753916733)

### Inferences
- For a live laptop dashboard with no Apple-side coding: TDInput (OSC into python-osc or any OSC receiver; OSC is not Mac-specific, so a Windows receiver should work even though the listing says "Mac") or Sensor Logger Phone & Watch mode with HTTP Push to a small Flask/FastAPI server (or MQTT to a broker) on the laptop.
- For recording then analysing: Care Sensors (up to 100 Hz device motion) or Sensor Logger exports.
- None of these apps documents end-to-end latency; all live paths except SensorLog's watch HTTP mode run through the iPhone.

### Gaps
- Whether Sensor Logger's "High frequency wrist motion" uses CMBatchedSensorManager and at what rate (no Hz in the listing or help page); whether its HTTP Push stream carries watch wrist motion live, or only after transfer (listing notes imply streaming mode includes watch data, but the help page doesn't say so explicitly).
- TDInput's watch-to-phone transport, and its rate for channels other than orientation, are not stated.

## Q4. What building a watch app requires; what a Windows-only user can do

### Takeaway
A custom watchOS app needs a Mac with Xcode (Xcode 27.x needs macOS Tahoe 26.6 or later), an iPhone paired to the watch (iPhone 11 / SE 2nd gen or later on iOS 27 for watchOS 27), Developer Mode on the watch, and an Apple Account. A free account (Personal Team) can install your own build on up to 3 of your devices with 7-day expiry, and HealthKit and Background Modes (needed for workout sessions) are available to free accounts on watchOS. The US$99/year Apple Developer Program adds TestFlight/App Store distribution (TestFlight builds last 90 days) and Xcode Cloud (25 compute hours/month), but Xcode Cloud must first be set up from Xcode. On Windows alone you cannot build or sideload a watch app; use App Store apps and write the receiver on Windows.

### Cited Findings
- Xcode 27, 27.1, 27.2 and 27.3 beta all require "macOS Tahoe 26.6 or later"; device support "watchOS 10 or later"; deployment targets watchOS 9-27 — [Apple: Xcode system requirements](https://developer.apple.com/xcode/system-requirements/)
- Free Personal Team: "You can register up to 10 App IDs, which expire after 7 days"; "up to 3 devices, which expire after 7 days"; "up to 3 apps per device. Provisioning profiles ... will expire 7 days from issuance. You'll need to rebuild and reinstall your app to your device after expiration" — [Apple: Compare memberships](https://developer.apple.com/support/compare-memberships/)

### Inferences
- Windows-only, no Mac: buy/install TDInput or Sensor Logger on an iPhone + Apple Watch and write the receiver (OSC listener, HTTP server or MQTT broker) on Windows. An iPhone is unavoidable because Apple Watch setup and updates require one.
- With a borrowed or rented Mac and a free account you can build and run your own watch app for 7 days at a time, enough for a hackathon; the watch and iPhone must be reachable from that Mac for installation, which a remote cloud Mac doesn't give you by default.
- With a paid account, a cloud Mac or Xcode Cloud (after a one-time Xcode setup) can deliver builds to your own iPhone/watch through TestFlight, avoiding a physical connection to the Mac.

### Gaps
- I did not verify a complete end-to-end cloud-Mac -> TestFlight -> Apple Watch install; forum users have reported TestFlight builds where the watch app did not install (not checked).
- No verified watchOS support in any Windows/Linux toolchain (xtool or others).

## Q5. CMMovementDisorderManager and HealthKit mobility/gait metrics: what they provide and whether third parties can use them

### Takeaway
CMMovementDisorderManager (watchOS 5+) returns per-minute tremor and dyskinesia likelihood summaries (not raw motion), requires an entitlement Apple must grant, is limited to clinically diagnosed patients, and keeps results 7 days. SensorKit (iOS 14+) can give research apps recorded accelerometer and rotation-rate data from the iPhone or paired watch, but only with an entitlement tied to a pre-approved research study. HealthKit mobility metrics (walking speed, step length, asymmetry, double support, walking steadiness, six-minute walk estimate, running form) are low-rate summary samples any HealthKit app can read with user permission; most walking metrics come from the iPhone, while the six-minute walk estimate and running form metrics come from the watch.

### Cited Findings
- CMMovementDisorderManager (watchOS 5.0+): measures "a resting Parkinsonian tremor in the 3-7 Hz range and choreiform dyskinetic symptoms"; wear the watch "on their most affected arm"; "requires an entitlement from Apple"; "Only collect data from patients clinically diagnosed with a movement disorder"; needs `NSMotionUsageDescription` — [Apple: CMMovementDisorderManager](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/coremotion/cmmovementdisordermanager); entitlement form — [Apple: Movement Disorder Entitlement Request](https://developer.apple.com/contact/request/movement-disorder-api-entitlement/)
- `monitorKinesias(forDuration:)`: results are stored "for a period of seven days after the time of recording"; "The maximum monitoring duration is seven days"; renew by calling again before expiry — [Apple: monitorKinesias(forDuration:)](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/coremotion/cmmovementdisordermanager/monitorkinesias(forduration:))
- CMTremorResult covers "a one-minute interval": percentUnknown + percentNone + percentSlight + percentMild + percentModerate + percentStrong = 1.0 — [Apple: CMTremorResult](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/coremotion/cmtremorresult); CMDyskineticSymptomResult (one minute): percentUnlikely + percentLikely = 1.0, shown only to users with choreiform dyskinesias — [Apple: CMDyskineticSymptomResult](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/coremotion/cmdyskineticsymptomresult)
- Apps must follow the "Movement Disorder API Addendum" ("only Apple Developer Program account holders can access the addendum") and show an intro screen covering purpose, data, use, background collection and opt-out — [Apple: Adhering to the movement disorder data collection requirements](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/coremotion/adhering-to-the-movement-disorder-data-collection-requirements)
- SensorKit (iOS 14+): "Retrieve data and derived metrics from sensors on an iPhone, or paired Apple Watch", including "Accelerometer or rotation-rate data"; entitlement `com.apple.developer.sensorkit.reader.allow` is "required by your app's preapproved research study" — [Apple: SensorKit](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/sensorkit); accelerometer and rotationRate sensors return `CMRecordedAccelerometerData` / `CMRecordedRotationRateData` — [Apple: SRSensor.accelerometer](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/sensorkit/srsensor/accelerometer); [Apple: SRSensor.rotationRate](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/sensorkit/srsensor/rotationrate); "An app has access to 7 days of prior recorded data for an active sensor", and fetch requests name "a device, such as a phone or a watch" — [Apple: SRSensorReader](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/sensorkit/srsensorreader)
- appleWalkingSteadiness: iPhone 8 or later carried near the waist, about one sample every 7 days, 0.0-1.0, read-only for apps — [Apple: appleWalkingSteadiness](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/healthkit/hkquantitytypeidentifier/applewalkingsteadiness)
- walkingSpeed, walkingStepLength, walkingAsymmetryPercentage, walkingDoubleSupportPercentage: recorded automatically on iPhone 8 or later near the waist, "10 to 30 ... samples on a typical day" — [walkingSpeed](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/healthkit/hkquantitytypeidentifier/walkingspeed); [walkingStepLength](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/healthkit/hkquantitytypeidentifier/walkingsteplength); [walkingAsymmetryPercentage](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/healthkit/hkquantitytypeidentifier/walkingasymmetrypercentage); [walkingDoubleSupportPercentage](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/healthkit/hkquantitytypeidentifier/walkingdoublesupportpercentage)
- sixMinuteWalkTestDistance: "On Apple Watch Series 3 or later, the system automatically records a weekly" estimate from passive motion and workout data; max 500 m; wear the watch at least 8 h/day, 3 days/week, at least 10 times over 4 weeks — [Apple: sixMinuteWalkTestDistance](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/healthkit/hkquantitytypeidentifier/sixminutewalktestdistance)
- runningGroundContactTime and runningStrideLength (watchOS 9+): "During outdoor running workouts, the system automatically records" them "on Apple Watch SE and Series 6 and later" — [Apple: runningGroundContactTime](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/healthkit/hkquantitytypeidentifier/runninggroundcontacttime); [Apple: runningStrideLength](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/healthkit/hkquantitytypeidentifier/runningstridelength)

### Inferences
- For a hackathon, CMMovementDisorderManager and SensorKit are out of reach (entitlement approval; diagnosed-patient or approved-study rules). HealthKit mobility metrics are readable without special entitlements, but they are daily or weekly summaries, not a motion stream; tremor-style analysis has to come from your own CMMotionManager data.

### Gaps
- How long Movement Disorder or SensorKit entitlement approval takes, and approval rates in 2026, are not published.
- Whether SensorKit's accelerometer/rotation-rate data on a given account comes from the watch, and at what rate, was not confirmed (docs say only that a phone or watch can be the fetch device).
