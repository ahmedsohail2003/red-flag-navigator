## Missing or contradicted

- **A Wii-remote-style grip needs Automatic Ear Detection turned off.** The notes cover ear-detection events but not what they mean for a hand-held controller. Apple's WWDC23 session says "You'll get a disconnect event when the buds are taken out of ear". Both Hack the North 2026 AirPods projects work around this. Poddle Helper says: "Take one AirPod out of your ear and hold it", and it requires "Automatic Ear Detection turned off, so motion keeps flowing when the AirPod is out of your ear". Aircade's app tells the user: "Turn off Automatic Ear Detection. Hold one earbud in a consistent grip." Its VERIFICATION.md records that hand-held input worked once the setting was off. This is the key technical step behind question (1). Sources: https://github.com/danielrltan/poddle-helper ; https://github.com/apollo-ullah/Aircade (DEVELOPMENT.md, VERIFICATION.md, Sources/Aircade/NeonRushView.swift) ; https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2023/10179/
- **iPhone, iPad and Mac apps need Local Network permission to send UDP to the laptop.** The notes recommend "one JSON or binary UDP datagram per sample ... to the PC's LAN IP" but never mention Apple's local network privacy. Apple's TN3179 says "Sending a UDP unicast" to a local address needs local network access. That rule applies on iOS 14+, iPadOS 14+ and macOS 15+, and the system shows an approval alert the first time. Apps should include `NSLocalNetworkUsageDescription`. If the user taps "Don't Allow", datagrams to the Windows PC are silently blocked. Not checked: whether Swift Playground offers a matching capability. Sources: https://developer.apple.com/documentation/technotes/tn3179-understanding-local-network-privacy ; https://developer.apple.com/documentation/bundleresources/information-property-list/nslocalnetworkusagedescription
- **OpenTrack wants degrees, but Core Motion gives radians.** Anyone writing their own OpenTrack sender must convert (multiply by 180/π). Byte order is little-endian on Windows, and the field order is x, y, z (cm), then yaw, pitch, roll. See the OpenTrack row above.
- **Sensor Logger is the fastest no-code route to Windows with full headphone fields, but it is batched.** The notes left this open. The push schema does include headphone motion with quaternion, rotation rate, gravity and acceleration, so it suits logging a walking test. Because it sends batches, it is a poor fit for real-time Wii-style control; for that use UDP per sample (Kishimen, or your own app).
- **Rate depends on the host.** On an iPhone, expect about 25 Hz, even with AirPods Pro 2 (TWSCardio, iOS 18). On a Mac, about 50 Hz has been reported (airpod-pose-estimate on macOS 26). The Aircade notes in htn_airpods_project.md report about 30 to 50 Hz on a Mac with a 20 ms timestamp grid; that was not re-checked here. The notes frame the difference as old AirPods versus new AirPods, which the evidence does not support.
- **Whether the AirPods must be the audio output is disputed** (Poddle Helper versus airpod-pose-estimate). Also, Aircade's in-app help says Bluetooth "can be connected while the motion stream is idle" and suggests disconnecting, reconnecting and briefly wearing the AirPods. Expect connection quirks, and build a reconnect button.
- **Question (3), home motor test: the notes are silent, but AirPods have been used for gait research.** arXiv 2503.13425 ("Movement Sequencing", 17 Mar 2025) had participants walk an outdoor circuit "while wearing a pair of AirPods", with orthopaedic braces to simulate pathology. Its features distinguished normal walking from brace walking at the population level. For a phone or AirPods walking test, the limits that matter are: about 25 Hz on iPhone; data from one bud at a time; packet loss when the phone is far from the AirPods or under ANC (TWSCardio); and background behaviour. Core Motion has no background support of its own (Apple DTS, thread 841001, August 2026). A phone-in-pocket test either keeps the app in the foreground with the screen on, or runs as a workout session (HKWorkoutSession) or another user-facing background mode, which should be tested. None of this is clinically validated for ALS. Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.13425
- **Android phones have no official way to read AirPods motion.** The only route is LibrePods, which needs root plus Xposed except on ColorOS/OxygenOS 16, realme UI 7.0 and Pixel on Android 16 QPR3. Its parsed output is only pitch, yaw and two acceleration values, and it is not offered to other apps.
- **The Apple Watch can read AirPods motion itself** (watchOS 11+, per Apple's June 2024 change log). The notes mention this, but the report should connect it to question (2): a watch app could log wrist and head motion together without an iPhone app in the loop. This was not tested here.
- **SmoothTrack's AirPods support appears only in old version notes**, not in the current description, which is about camera-based tracking. Check that AirPods Pro mode is still in the app before you pay £14.99 / $14.99.
- **Swift Playground (the new name) needs iPadOS 18+.** The kkebo project lists "Swift Playgrounds 4.3 or later" on iPadOS 16.1+, but the current 4.7 release needs iPadOS 18.0 or later, so older iPads are out.
