## Missing or contradicted

- **The notes do not answer who won Hack the North 2026 (question 1).** Hack the North has no overall first, second or third place. Its Devpost page says: "there's no first, second, or third place in our main award — top projects become finalists." The main award, "Hack the North 2026: Finalists", went to 12 projects: Reflex, Orbis Engine, Combadge, Composition, Concerto, CADEX, bingbong, Keymaeleon, Bricked, punching-face, TakeOne and Settlers of Solana. The official museum site lists the same 12 for 2026. Aircade was not a finalist; it won a sponsor prize, OpenAI: API Prizes, together with Orbis Engine (also a finalist) and Granny Waymo. Sources: https://hackthenorth2026.devpost.com/ , the gallery award data at https://hackthenorth2026.devpost.com/project-gallery , and https://museum.hackthenorth.com/ (all fetched 2026-11-07).
- **Aircade's placing within the OpenAI prize (1st, 2nd or 3rd) is still unverified.** The only hint is the ambiguous "placed 2nd on the leaderboard" member note on its Devpost page. Do not report it as 2nd place.
- **Windows (question 2).** Apple lists `CMHeadphoneMotionManager` only for Apple platforms, and both Hack the North projects need a Mac. A Windows 11 developer has three options:
  - A Mac reads the AirPods and forwards the data over the LAN. Poddle's hackathon bridge already listens on all interfaces at port 8787; Poddle Helper does not, since it binds to loopback only.
  - An iPhone app reads the AirPods with `CMHeadphoneMotionManager` (iOS 14+) and sends the data to the PC. Neither project ships one, and Aircade's iPhone app is installed from Xcode, which needs a Mac.
  - Phone-only motion through the browser's DeviceOrientation and DeviceMotion events, as Bearlympics did with Socket.IO. poddleball.com's phone-paddle mode also runs with a Windows PC browser.
- **Sample rate.** Apple documents no headphone-motion rate. The "50 Hz" figure and the "about 30 Hz delivered" figure both come from the teams' own Mac measurements. The rates in Apple's WWDC23 session belong to other APIs: 100 Hz is the `CMMotionManager` maximum, and 200 Hz device motion and 800 Hz accelerometer come from `CMBatchedSensorManager` on Apple Watch Series 8 and Ultra. None of them is a headphone rate. Neither repo measured AirPods motion on an iPhone. Source: https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2023/10179
- **Mac hardware caveat (unverified).** Apple's AirPods guide says Spatial Audio head tracking on a Mac needs "Apple silicon and macOS 12.3 or later". Apple's API page does not say whether `CMHeadphoneMotionManager` works on Intel Macs. Source: https://support.apple.com/guide/airpods/control-spatial-audio-and-head-tracking-dev00eb7e0a3/web
- **Head versus hand.** In both Hack the North projects the player holds the bud in the hand; nobody moves their head. If the user remembers players wearing the AirPods and nodding, that matches the MRU Hacks "AIRCADE" (Oct 2026), not Hack the North.
- **AI architecture.** The notes leave out how Aircade's AI side was built: a local Node.js and MongoDB station, the OpenAI Responses API (Astra), Vercel AI Gateway (Jev), and an optional Baseten logistic-regression opponent. Motion never leaves the Mac. This explains the vercel and baseten tags.
- **Leads for the home motor test (question 3) that the notes skip:**
  - RePlay (Hack the North 2026) built rehab games around an ESP32 with an MPU6050 IMU and a force sensor. The firmware runs a complementary filter, normalizes each axis to a per-patient range of motion, and streams 8 CSV channels at 115200 baud over Bluetooth Classic. A camera scores each rep. https://devpost.com/software/test-ohd6si
  - `CMHeadphoneActivityManager` (iOS 18+, macOS 15+, watchOS 11+) gives activity information "from headphone motion". https://developer.apple.com/documentation/coremotion/cmheadphoneactivitymanager
  - iOS 27 adds a SensorKit `headphoneMotion` stream (accelerometer, gyroscope rate, altitude), returned as `CMRecordedDeviceMotion`. SensorKit only works for research-study apps that Apple approves, with an entitlement. https://developer.apple.com/documentation/sensorkit/srsensor/headphonemotion ; https://developer.apple.com/documentation/sensorkit/configuring-your-project-for-sensor-reading
