## Missing or contradicted

- **The Fitbit Air and Edge both have gyroscopes but no developer access.** The notes list the Air's gyroscope as "not found"; Google's spec page says "3-axis accelerometer + gyroscope". For the 2026 devices, Google Health API data is the only developer path: steps at 1-minute resolution and heart rate at 1 s, after the device syncs. That suits a passive daily-activity measure, not gait or tremor analysis.
- **iPhone owners cannot use the suggested replacement watches.** No Pixel Watch supports iPhone ("Important: iPhones aren't supported"; https://support.google.com/googlepixelwatch/answer/12652073?hl=en). Samsung says "Newer Samsung watches with Wear OS are not compatible with iOS" (Galaxy Watch4, Watch5, Watch6, Watch7, Watch Ultra and Watch FE "rely on Google Play services which is not supported on iOS"; https://www.samsung.com/us/support/answer/ANS10004668/). If the user's phone is an iPhone, the notes' main recommendation (Pixel Watch or Galaxy Watch4 or later) does not work. Garmin or an Apple Watch would then be the options; Garmin's iOS support was not checked here.
- **A gyroscope app would need two separate Fitbit projects.** Getting Started says "It is not possible to build a single project for Fitbit OS 4 and Fitbit OS 5 simultaneously". The original Versa (OS 4, SDK 4.x) and the Sense (OS 5, SDK 5 to 6), the only two gyroscope targets, would need separate projects. The notes do not say this. It matters only for a watch that already has the app installed, given the login and install cutoffs.
- **Whether private-link installs survive 2026-11-28 is not stated.** Google's wording ("install or update third-party apps") does not exempt privately shared apps, but it does not name them either.
- **The help-page citations are slightly off.** The Google Maps and YouTube Music device lists are on help page 14237125 only, not on 14237121 as the notes imply. Both pages carry the 2026-11-28 notice. The notes also leave out that support for the legacy Web API ended on 2026-09-30, a date both Google sources state.
- **The Pixel Watch phone requirement depends on the model.** Android 12+ (Pixel Watch 5), 11+ (Pixel Watch 4), 10+ (Pixel Watch 3), 9+ (Pixel Watch 2) or 8+ (Pixel Watch). The notes' blanket "Android 12+" applies only to the Pixel Watch 5.
- **No direct route from a Fitbit to a laptop was found.** No documented Bluetooth (BLE) service lets a laptop read raw IMU data from any Fitbit. The notes do not look into this, and this check found none. Every documented route goes through the companion, which runs inside the phone's Google Health app.
- **The notes do not cover question 3 (a home motor test).** For the ALS phone walking test, nothing on the Fitbit side is usable for new work in late 2026. Fitbit OS app installs end on 2026-11-28 and the CLI login is gone, and the cloud API has no IMU data. A walking test should read the phone's own IMU (or a Wear OS watch on Android). Minute-level steps from the Google Health API could at most add a passive daily-activity covariate. This is an inference, not a sourced fact.
