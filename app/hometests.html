Home tests

Short weekly checks on a phone or laptop, one for each body region. They run alongside the EMG referral, never instead of it, and they never delay it. Each person is compared only with their own earlier results.

How and when

  • Once a week, same day, same time of day. Results can change within a single day (see the reading test), so the clock stays the same.
  • About 5 minutes in total: tapping with each hand, reading one passage aloud, and a weight.
  • Alongside the referral, not instead of it. If an EMG has been ordered, keep the appointment. The results go to the specialist with it.
  • What counts as a real change. The first two weekly results set the baseline. A change counts only when two results in a row are worse than the baseline by more than the test's normal variation (its minimal detectable change, MDC). One bad week never counts on its own.
  • What happens then. If the scorer put the patient in the Unclear group, the doctor decides by week : a real change, or signs in a new body region, means an EMG referral; otherwise the patient steps down. If an EMG is already booked, a real change is sent to the specialist, who can decide to see the patient sooner.

How it would work. The phone's motion sensor marks each step. The test reports stride-time variability: how much the time from one step of a foot to its next step changes, as a percentage of the average. Steadier walking gives a lower number, so here a rise is the warning sign.

Team calibration: how big a change is real?

Every test wobbles a little from one run to the next, even for a healthy person on the same evening. Before a drop is trusted, the team measures that wobble: each teammate does one test 10 times in a row, and the page works out the minimal detectable change.

The formula in plain words

  1. Each person's spread. Work out the standard deviation (SD) of each teammate's own trials. Everyone is compared only with themselves, so only this within-person spread matters.
  2. Pool it. Average the squared spreads (variances) across teammates and take the square root: the pooled within-person SD.
  3. Turn it into a change. MDC = 1.96 × √2 × pooled SD. The √2 is there because a change compares two results, and each has its own wobble; the 1.96 covers 95% of normal ups and downs.

A drop smaller than the MDC is normal variation.
