1. How the number is made

No AI produces the number. AI may only extract findings from clinic notes, and every finding must carry the exact sentence it came from, so a doctor can check it. A test checks every quote word for word against its note. The number itself is plain arithmetic on published study results.

    Why a range, not one number

    Two things are uncertain. The starting rate depends on age, and each odds ratio from the study has a 95% confidence interval. A single percentage would hide both. The range shows them, and the decision only needs to know one thing: does the whole range sit above the referral line, below it, or across it?

    The simulation uses a fixed seed, so the same findings always give the same range.

    3. The referral line is a values choice

    The line is not a statistic. It says how many normal EMGs a clinic is willing to order to find one person who needs neuromuscular care. That trade (the cost and wait of an EMG against a late diagnosis) is a judgement for the clinic and the patient, so the line is adjustable on the doctor dashboard. The validation study reports the results across all of these lines.

    4. Honest limits

      Home tests: what counts as a real drop

      Each person is compared with their own baseline, weekly, at a fixed time of day. A drop only counts if it is bigger than the test's minimal detectable change (MDC) twice in a row. Until the team's own repeat measurements are entered, the MDCs below are placeholders.

      6. Validation plan

      The written plan is in docs/VALIDATION.md. In short:

      1. Replay past records. Linked primary-care records (for example, Ontario primary-care EMR data linked to physician billing). Three groups: people later diagnosed with ALS; people with look-alike conditions (carpal tunnel, cervical myelopathy, radiculopathy, multifocal motor neuropathy, myasthenia, inclusion body myositis); and general controls. Notes are fed to the tool in date order, exactly as a family doctor would have seen them.
      2. Measure what matters. How many months earlier the tool would have escalated than the real referral date; EMG referrals per true case (and per neuromuscular diagnosis found); sensitivity at 6 and 12 months before diagnosis; discrimination (AUC, c-statistic); calibration (calibration-in-the-large, slope, plot); and decision curves across referral lines. Reported to TRIPOD+AI.
      3. Check the extraction. Every extracted finding is compared with a clinician's reading of the same note, and every quote must match its note word for word.
      4. Measure the home tests. MDC protocol: 10 repeats of each test per person, at the same time of day, to measure normal test-to-test variation.
      5. Silent pilot, then rollout. A silent prospective pilot in a family health team (the tool runs, nobody acts on it, outcomes are compared later), then a stepped rollout across sites.

      Regulation: likely regulated as software as a medical device in Canada (to be confirmed with Health Canada). The tool makes a referral recommendation; a clinician confirms it.
