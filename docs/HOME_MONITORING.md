Written 2026-11-08 for BioHacks 2026 (Red-Flag Navigator), for a judge or a teammate. It draws only on six research notes written and fact-checked the same night (blood pressure, heart rate and ECG; muscles, twitches, EIM and grip; microphone, camera and touch tasks; everyday tests, scales and exercise; rules by situation; data formats and browser limits) and on [WEARABLES.md](WEARABLES.md), which now covers 44 signals (30 at first, 14 added from these notes). Where WEARABLES.md covers a signal, this file points there instead of repeating it. Phone and watch motion sensors (gyroscope, accelerometer, gait and tremor from motion) belong to the gyro session and are not covered here.

Conventions. Every ALS group in every study below was already diagnosed unless the line says otherwise. "Computed by us" marks arithmetic done by the researchers, not a published number. MDC means minimal detectable change: the smallest change bigger than test noise at 95% confidence (MDD95 is the same idea). Grades describe the evidence that the signal changes in ALS: strong, moderate, weak, not studied, and contradicted (the evidence goes against it). Verdicts: **detects** (could help flag possible ALS before diagnosis, always through a clinician; the app shows this verdict as "Could flag (via a clinician)"), **monitors** (useful after diagnosis), **context** (worth showing, not evidence of ALS), **no use**.

## 1. The straight answer

### "Other than the gyros, what other diagnostics data is there ... what other kind of shit is actually going to detect anything?"

**Nothing you can measure at home has been shown to detect undiagnosed ALS.** Every home or device study we found enrolled people who were already diagnosed. What home data can do is show that one body region is getting weaker week after week, and that is what referral turns on: the Gold Coast criteria require progressive motor impairment shown by history or by repeated assessment ([Shefner 2020](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.clinph.2020.04.005)), and the MND Association red-flag tool asks "Is there progression?" before "refer" ([Red Flag tool](https://www.mndassociation.org/sites/default/files/public/2022-11/Red-Flag-tool-for-MND.pdf)).

Data type by data type:

- **Blood pressure: no help in finding ALS.** Hypertension diagnosed more than a year before diagnosis was not linked to motor neuron disease in 1,463 Swedish patients, 1,057 of them with ALS ([Chourpiliadis 2025](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.lanepe.2024.101173)). In 3,714 US Medicare patients with ALS it was slightly less common 2 years or more before diagnosis (OR 0.91) ([Abdel Magid 2022](https://doi.org/10.1159/000526982)). After diagnosis a lying-to-standing reading has a real job: orthostatic hypotension was found in 11.1% of 1,240 trial patients at baseline and 13.1% at follow-up ([Quarracino 2025](https://doi.org/10.3389/fneur.2025.1512357)), and it prompts a medication and fluid review.
- **Heart rate and HRV: no help in finding ALS.** In those 1,240 trial patients, resting heart rate rose only about 2 to 3 bpm over a median 8.8 months ([Quarracino 2025](https://doi.org/10.3389/fneur.2025.1512357)), well inside the single-day noise of about 8.4 bpm (computed in WEARABLES.md). HRV is lower in diagnosed ALS and fell further over 12 months in newly diagnosed people ([Maset-Roig 2024](https://doi.org/10.3390/s24072355); [Dubbioso 2023](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00415-023-11832-w)), but no study shows it changing before diagnosis. Context only.
- **ECG: no help in finding ALS.** Holter ECGs were normal in all 35 patients of a cardiac MRI study, even when the MRI showed changes ([Rosenbohm 2017](https://doi.org/10.3389/fneur.2017.00479)), and atrial fibrillation was slightly less common 24 months or more before diagnosis (OR 0.81) ([Abdel Magid 2022](https://doi.org/10.1159/000526982)). Its one real home job is drug safety: the US label for dextromethorphan/quinidine asks for an ECG at baseline and 3 to 4 hours after the first dose in people at risk of QT prolongation ([NUEDEXTA label](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=c8cce784-8c5f-415b-8068-76e5d481d159)).
- **Twitches (fasciculations): one of the biggest red flags, but only when a GP writes them down.** GP-recorded "muscle fasciculations or twitching" 3 months to 5 years before diagnosis carried an adjusted OR of 40.2 in 6,437 people later diagnosed with MND against 62,003 controls ([Mei 2022](https://doi.org/10.1136/bmjopen-2021-058383)). No home device is validated to count them, and healthy people twitch: surface electrodes found twitches in 77% of 122 healthy adults ([Mitsikostas 1998](https://doi.org/10.1002/(sici)1097-4598(199804)21:4%3C533::aid-mus14%3E3.0.co;2-y)). The app should ask about twitching and prompt a GP examination.
- **Specialist ultrasound and research surface EMG: they detect, in a clinic, after referral.** Ultrasound found twitches with pooled sensitivity 0.87 and specificity 0.91 across 13 studies ([Tang 2026](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00415-026-13775-4)). A 30-minute 64-electrode biceps recording separated 39 people with ALS from 25 controls with AUC 0.89 ([Tamborska 2020](https://doi.org/10.1093/braincomms/fcaa141)). Home versions are in trials with no results ([RHAALS](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT06046599)).
- **EIM (electrical impedance myography): monitors in trials, does not detect, cannot be bought.** It did not separate ALS from mimics (AUC 0.60) ([Shefner 2018](https://doi.org/10.1080/21678421.2018.1510008)); the consumer scanner used at home was discontinued ([Skulpt](https://www.skulpt.me/)).
- **Weight: the strongest passive signal**, and it feeds nutrition decisions after diagnosis: 67.5% of 2,420 Dutch patients had lost weight by diagnosis ([Janse van Mantgem 2020](https://doi.org/10.1136/jnnp-2020-322909)). See "Body weight" in WEARABLES.md.
- **Region-specific active tests: the closest thing to detection.** A weekly test of one region can document progression in an unclear case: phone tapping for the hand, reading aloud for speech and a timed walk for the legs (WEARABLES.md). Speaking rate fell 1.74 wpm a month in spinal onset and 4.16 in bulbar onset in lab tests ([Eshghi 2022](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9489769/)), and home walking speed fell significantly in 5 of 9 people, by an average of 0.021 m/s a month ([Lukac](https://doi.org/10.1159/000530067)); tapping was lower than in controls, but scores rose with practice over 24 weeks and no net decline was shown ([WatchALS](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13423962/)). Tonight's additions are syllable repetition (WatchALS's 15-s iPhone version had a weekly home MDC of 1.03 syllables per second ([WatchALS, Burke 2026](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13423962/)); the app's 10-s browser version is not validated), and the stopwatch tests and grip below.
- **Breathing:** consumer SpO2, breathing rate and sleep are context (WEARABLES.md). Maximum phonation time and the single-breath count are cheap breathing proxies after diagnosis that prompt a real cough-flow or vital-capacity test ([Tabor Gray 2023](https://doi.org/10.1044/2022_JSLHR-22-00522); [Quinn 2021](https://doi.org/10.1080/21678421.2021.1915337)).
- **Camera: not ready for ALS.** No ALS study of hand video was found; face video has one small ALS study with a depth camera ([Guarin 2022](https://doi.org/10.1044/2022_JSLHR-22-00072)); phone eye tracking has one 10-person feasibility study in ALS ([Barahim Bastani 2024](https://doi.org/10.1159/000538992)).
- **Cognition and behaviour: they matter, but they are not motor evidence.** About half of people with ALS have some change and 12.6% to 13.8% have frontotemporal dementia ([Phukan 2012](https://doi.org/10.1136/jnnp-2011-300188); [Montuschi 2015](https://doi.org/10.1136/jnnp-2013-307223)). A clinician screens with the ECAS after diagnosis.
- **No drop is not reassurance.** Within an 8-week window most signals catch only fast decliners: an average decliner needs about 8.6 months for gait speed to fall past its 0.18 m/s MDC, against about 1.3 months for a fast decliner heading for a walking aid (computed in WEARABLES.md, "Eight weeks is short", from [Lukac](https://doi.org/10.1159/000530067) and [WatchALS](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13423962/)).

### "What are the everyday exercises and what do you give based on the situation?"

- **Everyday tests (section 3).** One test per body region is allowed to escalate a case: walking speed for the legs, phone tapping for the hand and reading rate for speech, plus body weight for the whole body (the engine's HOME_TESTS). For the legs, the score counts the phone walking test on the Monitoring page (HOME_TESTS.gait_speed, merged by the main session on 8 Nov). The kit's stopwatch walk measures the same walk when the phone's sensors cannot be used, and is context only, so one leg decline is never counted twice. Everything else (TUG, chair stands, grip, copy typing, syllable repetition, reading pauses, the breathing tasks and the questionnaires) is context or monitoring. Two practice sessions come before the baseline, and the baseline restarts after a new brace, cane, walker, hand surgery or illness. Five tests that can each escalate would give a stable person about a 20% false-alarm chance over 8 weeks (computed by us in the everyday-tests notes, simulation with home noise 1.5 times the published noise).
- **Exercise after diagnosis is treatment, not a test.** Regular moderate exercise is probably beneficial and not harmful in early ALS (level B, [Canadian guideline, Shoesmith 2020](https://doi.org/10.1503/cmaj.191721)). Twelve small trials (430 people) found a modest gain in function (SMD 0.50) with high risk of bias ([Ren 2025](https://doi.org/10.3389/fneur.2025.1499407)); Cochrane rates the evidence low ([Dal Bello-Haas 2013](https://doi.org/10.1002/14651858.CD005229.pub3)). The clinic physiotherapist sets the programme; section 3.6 gives the dose and the warning signs.
- **What to give by situation (section 4).** Low suspicion: no home tests, only a safety-net checklist. Unclear: a weekly test of the affected region, a screening test of each other region every 2 weeks, weight weekly, a weekly falls question, and the 8-week decision; only the main tests (finger tapping and reading aloud on the Monitoring page, and the timed walk) can document progression, and the kit's other tests are context. Before diagnosis a red flag comes first: breathless at rest or lying flat means contact the doctor today, and trouble swallowing (or an EAT-10 of 3 or more) means contact the doctor now. High (referred, waiting): keep going, so that a worsening trend can move the referral up. Diagnosed: the guideline domains (nutrition, breathing, swallowing, speech, mobility, hand, cognition) at the guideline intervals, each with its action trigger. Late stage: breathing support, communication aids and palliative care.

## 3. Everyday tests and exercises by body region

General rules for every test (our synthesis in the everyday-tests notes, built on the ALS exercise consensus): do two practice sessions before the baseline counts; average 2 or 3 tries where the test allows; use the same shoes, brace, aid, chair and device every week, and start a new baseline when any of them changes; stop if dizzy, short of breath, unsteady, in pain or cramping; tell the clinic about any fall. The stopwatch and peg-test MDCs come from supervised or video-watched sessions, so unsupervised home noise is probably larger; the WatchALS and home-grip figures were measured at home.

Falls are common and early: 47% of walkers fell within 3 months ([Alencar 2026](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.bjpt.2026.101623)), and 57.6% of 224 people fell within 12 months ([Hu 2026](https://doi.org/10.1159/ndd/aduag003)). So walking, TUG and chair tests are done only with someone in the room if the person has fallen or nearly fallen in the last 3 months, uses a walking aid, or has a TUG of 14 s or more (our rule), and standing tests stop once the person cannot stand up without help.

### 3.6 Exercise after diagnosis (treatment, set by the clinic physiotherapist)

- **What helps.** Moderate exercise in early ALS is probably beneficial and well tolerated (level B), with a personalised strength and aerobic programme for those able to take part; "moderate" means sweating a little and breathing harder ([Shoesmith 2020](https://doi.org/10.1503/cmaj.191721)). NICE and the EAN advise a programme fitted to function and fatigue, with carers taught to help ([NICE NG42 1.8.5 to 1.8.8](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng42/chapter/Recommendations); [EAN 2024](https://doi.org/10.1111/ene.16264)).
- **Dose.** Resistance: a weight you can lift comfortably 20 times, 2 to 3 sets of 10; do not train muscles that cannot lift against gravity; avoid lowering-under-load (eccentric) work. Aerobic: moderate, a recumbent bike rather than a treadmill because of falls, starting with 10-minute bouts 2 to 3 times a week; if you cannot talk comfortably, it is too hard. Balance work only with a physiotherapist. Daily stretching from early on ([Majmudar 2014](https://doi.org/10.1002/mus.24202)).
- **Warning signs.** Fatigue or pain lasting more than 30 minutes after exercise, or getting in the way of daily life, means the programme needs changing ([Shoesmith 2020](https://doi.org/10.1503/cmaj.191721); [Majmudar 2014](https://doi.org/10.1002/mus.24202)). Our additions: new falls or new breathlessness mean pause and tell the clinic; worsening cramps are a reason for supervised rather than home exercise, since supervised exercise eased cramp pain and unsupervised home exercise did not ([Hall 2025](https://doi.org/10.1159/000548820)).
- **Breathing muscle training** raised expiratory pressure by about 21 cmH2O against -1.0 with sham, and FVC did not change, in 45 people with early ALS ([Plowman 2023](https://doi.org/10.1212/WNL.0000000000206830)). Neither the EAN nor the NICE guideline mentions it; a clinician sets the load.
- **As weakness grows** (our synthesis): seated or recumbent cycling instead of walking exercise, an ankle-foot orthosis without delay, then an active-assisted programme with a carer, then passive range of motion, positioning and cough help. For a weak cough, NICE starts with breath stacking or a manual assisted cough, then a lung volume recruitment bag, then a mechanical cough assist ([NICE NG42 1.14.1 to 1.14.4](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng42/chapter/Recommendations)). No exercise trial has included wheelchair users or late-stage ALS.

## 4. Rules by situation

Thresholds are quoted as the guideline publishes them. "Our rule" marks an app rule built on a guideline, not a guideline statement. No guideline we opened supports waiting once MND is suspected: NICE says refer without delay and name the possible diagnosis in the letter ([NICE NG42 1.1.4](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng42/chapter/Recommendations)).

**Red flags come first.** Before diagnosis, the app shows a red flag it already knows about as an alert that says what to do, not only as an "If this happens" line. Breathless at rest or when lying flat means "contact your doctor today": the alert appears when breathing trouble is in the visit notes or the person's answers, or after a Yes to either of those questions in the weekly breathing check. Trouble swallowing means "contact your doctor now": the alert appears when the notes record it, or after an EAT-10 of 3 or more is saved. The alert after saving a breathing check or an EAT-10 shows after diagnosis too. This follows NICE for progressive weakness or slurred speech: refer immediately if breathless at rest or lying flat, and urgently with any swallowing impairment ([NICE NG127 1.7.5 and 1.13.2](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng127/chapter/Recommendations-for-adults)).

## 5. What the app now does

**The Home plan view** (the "Home plan" tab, `app/js/app/views/homeplan.js`, route `#plan`). It works for any case in the Case switcher. Browser checks on 8 Nov 2026, at about 04:45:
- Alex at two visits, with Home data off and on;
- all 8 built-in cases render without errors. The 5 low-suspicion look-alikes get no tests, only safety-net advice, as the rules say.
- The view's reviewer separately checked a user-built case with no visits.

It shows, top to bottom:
1. **The situation:** the stage (low, unclear, high or diagnosed), the regions affected so far, the 8-week window and the reason, from the same engine as the score. A few answers shape the plan: which devices the person has, whether they can walk safely, recent falls, a walking aid, needing their arms to stand, and breathing symptoms, with breathlessness lying flat asked on its own.
2. **This week's plan:** before diagnosis, any red flag the app already knows about comes first, as an alert to contact the doctor today or now (section 4). Then what to do weekly, every 2 weeks and monthly, and what to collect automatically. Each item says why. Then the "If this happens, then..." triggers, each with its source, and the safety rules. Walking and chair tests switch off after a fall or foot drop in the notes, or a fall or near-fall in the last 3 months (the person's answer, the weekly falls question or a watch), until the person says they can walk safely.
3. **The tests,** run inline: syllable repetition, holding "aah", one-breath count and reading pauses by microphone; copy typing; a stopwatch walk, chair stands and up-and-go; the swallowing, breathing and falls questions; grip from a dynamometer. Practice runs stay out of the baseline.
4. **The person's data by body region:** a weekly chart with the normal-variation band, the status in words ("Decline confirmed in the week of ...", "Within normal variation", "Not judged" when no MDC is published), the trend per month, and labels: Real, Imported, Entered, Simulated, Context only, Not validated, and Assumption (a placeholder noise figure, as on tapping and reading). Context-only rows such as blood pressure never say "Decline confirmed": they say the value changed beyond normal variation and is not a sign of ALS on its own.
5. **Bringing in device data:** an Apple Health export (zip or xml, also by drag and drop), CSV (generic, or Withings weight and blood pressure), typed-in readings (blood pressure, heart rate, SpO2, weight, grip, temperature, peak cough flow, FVC), and standard Bluetooth devices. Delete by source. An import drops anything older than 12 weeks before the first visit (12 weeks before today when there are no visits), and "Your data" starts at the same date, so the baseline comes from the weeks before the visit, never from years back. The 12 weeks are our choice; WEARABLES.md advises a recent window with the weeks just before the visit as the baseline.
6. **"What actually detects anything":** the app's version of the verdict table in section 2, where "detects" reads "Could flag (via a clinician)" and body weight is "monitors".
7. **Data handling:** export as CSV, or copy it, and delete everything in two clicks.

In the hosted copy, the microphone, Bluetooth and downloads are refused, and the page says so in plain words. The imports work there. Simulated data for Alex appears only with Home data switched on, and is written to the browser only when "Load demo data" is pressed.

**Built tonight, with tests** (the files in `app/js/home/`):

- **Microphone timing tools** (`audio.js`): syllable repetition rate, maximum phonation time, single-breath count and pause time while reading, measured from the browser microphone. They are timing tools, not validated.
- **Copy typing** (`typing.js`): six everyday sentences; it keeps key timings only, never the typed text. Not validated.
- **Imports:** an Apple Health export (`import/applehealth.js`, streamed so a multi-gigabyte export never sits in memory; 25 HealthKit types into 24 signals, from steps and walking speed to blood pressure, SpO2 and sleep), read through a zip reader (`import/zip.js`); CSV files in a generic date, signal and value format or as Withings weight and blood pressure exports, typed-in readings (blood pressure, heart rate, SpO2, weight, grip, temperature, peak cough flow, FVC) and CSV export (`import/csv.js`); and standard Bluetooth heart-rate straps, blood pressure cuffs, scales and pulse oximeters (`import/bluetooth.js`).
- **Storage** (`store.js`): samples and results stay in this browser (`rfn.home.samples`, `rfn.home.results`); nothing leaves the device, and data can be deleted by source.
- **Weekly series and the decline rule** (`series.js`): weekly values (the median by default), a baseline after the practice weeks, and a decline when results are worse than baseline by more than the MDC for consecutive weeks.
- The existing home tests (`app/js/hometests.js`: tapping each hand, reading, weight, walk) stay as they are; the kit only reads them.

**Also built and reviewed, each with its own test file:**
- `catalog.js`: the sourced catalogue of tests, device signals, post-diagnosis action triggers and verdicts, every number with its source;
- `protocol.js`: the situation from the engine's evaluation, and the plan for it (weekly, every 2 weeks, monthly and passive), with its triggers and safety rules;
- `summary.js`: the person's data by body region, for the view;
- `demo.js`: Alex's simulated demo data.

## 6. Limits and pitfalls

- **Every cohort was already diagnosed**, often years earlier. None of these tests has been validated for picking out undiagnosed ALS.
- **Eight weeks is short.** Most signals catch only fast decliners: at the average rate, gait speed takes about 8.6 months to fall past its MDC (computed in WEARABLES.md, "Eight weeks is short", from [Lukac](https://doi.org/10.1159/000530067) and [WatchALS](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13423962/)). No decline is weak evidence and never rules ALS out.
- **More escalating tests mean more false alarms.** One test per region escalates; with home noise 1.5 times the published noise, five such tests would flag about 20% of stable people over 8 weeks (computed by us).
- **Practice effects** raise scores for weeks and hide decline; two practice sessions come before the baseline ([WatchALS](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13423962/); [Knak 2017](https://doi.org/10.2340/16501977-2222)).
- **No stopwatch test, grip or peg test has its own MDC measured in ALS** (the timed walk borrows WatchALS's 60-s walk), and the stopwatch and peg-test MDCs come from supervised or video-watched sessions, so home noise is probably larger. One unsupervised home device in ALS, a knee dynamometer, did match supervised devices (SEM 5.8%) ([van Unnik 2023](https://doi.org/10.1080/21678421.2023.2231494)).
- **Fixed on 8 Nov: the Monitoring page's tests used the engine's old placeholder MDCs** (6 taps, 15 wpm and 2 kg for weight). The engine now uses the recommended figures (18 taps, 31 wpm, 2.4 kg, 0.28 m/s). WEARABLES.md showed the old ones were too small: at these placeholders about 44% to 62% of stable people would get at least one false decline across both hands, reading and weight within 8 weeks (computed in WEARABLES.md, "Our placeholder MDCs", assuming independent tests and weeks). The engine takes other MDCs through `opts.mdc`, but today only the team's own calibration fills it, never the recommended values.
- **A twitch count cannot use the worse-than-baseline rule**: in weak muscles twitch rates fell by 7.6 a minute each month ([Bashford 2020, rise and fall](https://doi.org/10.1093/braincomms/fcaa018)), so a falling count can mean a worsening muscle.
- **The breathing proxies depend on effort, instructions and speech.** A short MPT or count prompts a real test; it never changes the referral score.
- **Pulse oximeters** can under- or over-estimate, especially near borderline values, and over-estimation has been reported in people with dark skin ([NICE NG42 note at 1.15.8](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng42/chapter/Recommendations)).
- **Consumer blood pressure features are not built for this**: cuff-calibrated watches and bracelets lose accuracy as blood pressure moves away from the calibration value, and Samsung Canada says not to use its feature with a neurological disorder such as tremor ([Samsung Canada](https://www.samsung.com/ca/apps/samsung-health-monitor/)). More than 85% of home monitors sold worldwide are not accurate, per a CMAJ patient guide ([Mostarac 2021](https://doi.org/10.1503/cmaj.210268)).
- **Licences.** Every electronic ALSFRS-R needs a licence for every user ([ePROVIDE, ALSFRS-R](https://eprovide.mapi-trust.org/instruments/amyotrophic-lateral-sclerosis-functional-rating-scale-revised-version)); the EAT-10 needs a licence agreement even for free academic use and must not be altered ([Nestlé Nutrition Institute](https://nestlenutrition-institute.org/resources/nutrition-tools/details/swallowing-assessment-tool)); the ECAS form is copyrighted and app licensing is not confirmed ([ECAS form](https://era.ed.ac.uk/bitstreams/6d91846b-1215-4efa-b5c2-ca6ca41b6629/download)).
- **Browser limits.** The microphone and camera need HTTPS ([MDN getUserMedia](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/MediaDevices/getUserMedia)); the Web Speech API sends audio to a server by default, so scoring does not rely on it ([MDN Web Speech API](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/Web_Speech_API/Using_the_Web_Speech_API)); Web Bluetooth works in Chrome and Edge but has no built-in support on iOS, in Safari or in Firefox ([implementation status](https://github.com/whatwg/bluetooth/blob/main/implementation-status.md)).
- **Apple Health export quirks**: dates are local time plus an offset, not ISO 8601; percentages are fractions (SpO2 0.97 means 97%); each blood pressure reading appears twice; exports reach hundreds of megabytes, so the file must be streamed (data-formats notes, from open-source parsers such as [healthkit-to-sqlite](https://github.com/dogsheep/healthkit-to-sqlite/blob/main/healthkit_to_sqlite/utils.py) and [MDN on string limits](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/JavaScript/Reference/Global_Objects/String/length)).
- **Restart the baseline** after a new brace, cane, walker, hand surgery or illness.
- **Not found:** an ALS test-retest MDC for any stopwatch test, grip, peg test or EAT-10; a home twitch-count device with results; any exercise trial in wheelchair users; the licence terms for the CNS Bulbar Function Scale and Dyspnea-12; the ROADS licence text.
