# Phone tests track ALS; wearables add context

> **Update, 8 Nov 2026 (after the merges, about 06:00).** Parts of this file describe the engine as it was before.
> - The engine's default MDCs are now the recommended figures: 18 taps per 10 s (provisional), 31 wpm, 2.4 kg, and 0.28 m/s for walking speed. They are no longer the old 6 taps, 15 wpm and 2 kg.
> - The 10 m phone walking test is built and merged as `HOME_TESTS.gait_speed`. It has been checked on synthetic and replayed walks only, not yet on a real phone.
> - The research now covers 44 signals: 7 strong, 20 moderate, 13 weak and 4 not studied.
> - Not done: the two practice weeks before the baseline.

**No phone or wearable signal has been validated for detecting undiagnosed ALS.** Every device cohort we found had already been diagnosed, often years earlier: WatchALS participants averaged 51.9 months of disease ([Burke 2026, WatchALS](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13423962/)). What phones can add is a weekly, region-by-region check of whether a weakness is getting worse: finger tapping for the hand, reading aloud for speech, a timed walk for the legs. Smart scales and wearables add stored history, such as years of weight or walking-speed data, which can give an unclear patient a baseline from before the visit. After diagnosis they add trends tied to real decisions: weight for nutrition and gastrostomy, breathing tests for non-invasive ventilation (NIV), walking for mobility aids. They add almost nothing passive for the hand, speech or swallowing. Health Connect, the Google Health API and Withings have no such measure, and Apple's keyboard and speech metrics are research-only ([Health Connect data types](https://developer.android.com/health-and-fitness/guides/health-connect/plan/data-types); [Google Health API data types](https://developers.google.com/health/data-types); [Apple SensorKit](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/sensorkit)). Passive signals such as steps, heart rate and sleep are not specific to ALS, and their normal swings (season, holidays, a new cane) are bigger than the change an 8-week window can show. **Within 8 weeks most signals will catch only fast decliners, so no drop is not reassurance.**

**For the demo, show four signals: phone finger tapping, reading rate, body weight and a timed walk scored as gait speed.** They are the engine's existing home tests, fixed. Tapping and reading already work in the app, weight is a working entry, and the walk is a mockup today. Three of them have published test-retest noise from weekly home testing in people with ALS, and weight has a sourced noise estimate from healthy adults. Three are specific to one body region, and each maps onto a dated finding in Alex's notes. Their placeholder MDCs (minimal detectable changes) are too small. Under the engine's own rule, the 6-tap threshold would flag a false decline in about 21% to 33% of stable people per hand over 8 weekly tests (computed by us, with noise taken from WatchALS and independent weeks; see the section on placeholder MDCs). We recommend about 18 taps, 31 words per minute (wpm) before diagnosis and 22 after, 0.28 m/s before diagnosis and 0.18 after, and 2.4 kg, plus two practice weeks before the baseline. We cover 44 signals in all: 7 graded strong, 20 moderate, 13 weak and 4 not studied (30 at first, 14 added on 8 Nov).

## Device data enters the engine three ways, never as a Mei odds ratio

The engine (`app/js/engine.js`) reads home tests as rows of `{ date, test, value, simulated }` and ignores any test without a `HOME_TESTS` entry. `HOME_TESTS` has five today: `tap_right` and `tap_left` (taps in 10 s, region arm, placeholder MDC 6), `walk` (stride-time variability in percent, region leg, MDC 1.0), `reading` (words per minute, region bulbar, MDC 15) and `weight` (kg, region general, MDC 2). `detectDecline` needs at least 4 weekly points. The baseline is the mean of the first two. A decline is two consecutive points, each worse than that baseline by more than the MDC. There is no practice period. A measured MDC can replace a placeholder through `opts.mdc`.

A decline creates a `home_decline` finding (assumed weight 3.0, range 1.5 to 6.0, motor) in the test's region. Motor findings in arm, leg, bulbar or breathing count toward `motorRegions`, so a home decline in a second region fires `multi_region` and goes straight to "refer for EMG". In an "unclear" case, any home decline fires `unclear_with_decline` and escalates. That includes a weight decline: region "general" does not count as a motor region, but it still escalates an unclear case. **So a noisy test can send a patient to EMG on its own.** Every device signal that feeds the engine needs an honest region and an MDC that keeps false alarms rare over 8 weekly tests.

There are three routes in. **Route 1, a weekly test.** Region-specific active tests enter as `HOME_TESTS` entries. A passive signal could join later, but only after it is reduced to one weekly value (for example the weekly median, with a minimum number of valid days) and its weekly noise is known. **Route 2, a prompt to the clinician.** A scale-confirmed weight loss, a watch-recorded fall or a high swallowing score prompts the clinician to ask and record the symptom in a note. Only then does the note finding (`weight_loss`, `falls` or `dysphagia`) carry its Mei weight, because those weights come from symptoms recorded by GPs ([Mei 2022](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9240876/)). **Route 3, context only.** Steps, heart rate, HRV, sleep, temperature and the consumer breathing signals sit beside the score. They never become a motor-region decline.

Stored history could give an unclear patient a baseline from before the visit instead of waiting weeks, but each platform limits it. HealthKit keeps years of data on the iPhone, but from iOS 27 a user can grant an app only a recent window. `getEarliestAuthorizedSampleDate(for:)` reports the limit, and Apple tells apps to treat earlier data as unknown ([Apple: authorizing access](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/healthkit/authorizing-access-to-health-data)). Health Connect gives an app only the 30 days before its first permission grant unless it also holds `READ_HEALTH_DATA_HISTORY` ([Health Connect: read data](https://developer.android.com/health-and-fitness/guides/health-connect/develop/read-data)). The Google Health API puts no limit on how much history an app may read ([Google Health API data types](https://developers.google.com/health/data-types)). Withings keeps history if a Withings+ membership lapses, though sharing pauses until it is renewed ([Withings plans](https://static.withings.com/content/api/plans/plans.html)). Garmin's free Starter tier delivers no history, Enterprise adds at least a month of backfill, and the Personal tier's window is not stated ([Garmin program overview](https://developer.garmin.com/gc-developer-program/overview/); [DC Rainmaker](https://www.dcrainmaker.com/2026/10/garmin-connect-api-revamps-connect-developer.html)). SensorKit holds new data for 24 hours ([Apple: SRFetchRequest](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/sensorkit/srfetchrequest)) and offers only 7 days of prior data ([Apple: SRSensorReader](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/sensorkit/srsensorreader)). One build warning: `detectDecline` takes the first two points as the baseline and returns the first qualifying pair it finds, so the flag stays on at every later date. Fed two years of weekly medians, it would compare everything with a week two years ago and could lock in a flag from an old injury or holiday. Feed a recent window, with the weeks just before the visit as the baseline.

## Ranking rules: region first, then noise, history and access

**(a) Earlier referral, while a case is "unclear".** We ranked on six criteria, roughly in this order of weight: specificity for motor decline in one body region; noise compared with the change expected within the 8-week window; whether past data already exists; whether it can be accessed now without special approval; evidence grade; and equity (cost and platform).

**(b) Monitoring after diagnosis.** We ranked on responsiveness over months; links to clinical decisions (NIV, nutrition and gastrostomy, mobility aids); evidence grade; and burden and adherence.

**Evidence grades** describe the evidence that the signal changes in ALS, not its validity for screening. **Strong:** replicated in two or more independent ALS cohorts, or one large registry or cohort plus supporting studies, measured the way a phone, wearable or home device would measure it, or as the same physical quantity (for example body weight). **Moderate:** at least one longitudinal or case-control ALS study of about 20 or more people with ALS using a comparable device or task, or several smaller consistent studies. **Weak:** only small pilots, cross-sectional differences, clinic-grade measurements whose consumer version is unvalidated in ALS, or indirect evidence. **Not studied:** no ALS study found; we give the closest evidence from other conditions, labelled as such. Two judgement calls follow. Research wrist sensors such as the ActiGraph count as wearables, so wrist evidence can be strong. Clinic oximeters, sleep labs and DXA count as clinic-grade, so consumer SpO2, sleep staging and body composition are graded weak even where the clinic evidence in ALS is strong.

**Eight weeks is short.** An average decliner needs about 8.6 months for gait speed to fall past the 0.18 m/s MDC, against about 1.3 months for a fast decliner heading for a walking aid (computed by us from [Lukac](https://doi.org/10.1159/000530067) and [WatchALS](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13423962/)). A tapping drop of 33% to 35% takes about 7 to 17 months at the monthly rates seen for hand function, 2.1% to 4.9% (computed by us from [Straczkiewicz 2024, JNER](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11662782/) and [Straczkiewicz 2024, eBioMedicine](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10914560/)). Limb-onset reading rate falls about 0.3 to 3.2 wpm in 8 weeks, far below its 22 to 31 wpm MDC (computed by us from [Eshghi 2022](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9489769/) and [Kothare 2023](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11246071/)). Weight lost at 0.64 kg a month (the median loss before the first visit in Shimizu 2012, a BMI fall of 2.5 a year, at a height of 1.75 m) takes about 3.7 months to pass 2.35 kg (computed by us from [Shimizu 2012](https://doi.org/10.3109/17482968.2012.678366) and [Bhutani 2017](https://doi.org/10.14814/phy2.13336)). Even the ALSFRS-R fine-motor subscore moves only about 0.4 points in 8 weeks, below its 1-point step (computed by us from [Straczkiewicz 2024, JNER](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11662782/)). A flag inside 8 weeks is meaningful; its absence is weak evidence. The engine steps an unclear case down after 8 weeks without a decline. Its message should say "no decline seen; this does not rule out ALS" and keep the safety-net advice.

They are also the top four signals for earlier referral, and weight is the top signal for monitoring. **Why.** The right hand is Alex's first region: handwriting fading in June 2024, then weaker grip and a "little flatter" thumb muscle on 14 Oct 2024. Add one line: "Carpal tunnel syndrome also slows tapping, so a tapping drop cannot tell the two apart" ([Seok 2019](https://doi.org/10.1002/mus.26424)).

**Real and simulated.** Real: the test, its scoring, Alex's note findings and their dates. Simulated: Alex's tap counts (right 54 falling to 46; left about 60 to 62). Provisional: the 18-tap MDC, scaled by us from WatchALS, whose MDC column may be in taps or percent.

**Alex's timeline.** With the 6-tap placeholder, the simulated right-hand series flags a decline on 2 Dec 2024 (baseline 54.5, latest 47). At the 10% referral line that turns the tier "high" a week before the hand-surgeon visit. With 18 taps, or with the first two weeks treated as practice, there is no flag. The referral then comes on 9 Dec 2024 from the `two_territory` and `unexplained_weakness` rules. The headline does not change, because it already excludes home tests: 14.3 months earlier than the real referral at the 10% line. With home tests switched on and the 6-tap placeholder, the dashboard's simulated figure would read 14.5 months at that line (checked). Show the honest version. The home test runs alongside the referral and does not drive it. If the team wants to show what a flag looks like, use a separate series labelled "hypothetical faster decliner". After his carpal tunnel release on 20 Jan 2025, the right-hand baseline would need to restart.

### 2. Reading rate (speech)

**Why.** Strong evidence, a working test, and an ALS-specific MDC ([WatchALS](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13423962/)). Speech is how spread to a third region would show, and Alex's own clinic numbers prove why the protocol matters.

**What the screen shows.** The live reading test. A callout with Alex's real clinic values from 22 Sep 2026: 165 wpm at 9 am and 118 wpm at 4 pm. That 47-wpm gap (computed by us) is about twice the 22-wpm MDC, so the test must run at the same time of day every week. Then a weekly line with the MDC band, labelled simulated.

**Real and simulated.** Real: the test, Alex's clinic values and the note quotes. Simulated: any weekly reading series for Alex.

**Alex's timeline.** On 16 Feb 2026 his partner said he was "quieter in the evenings" (recorded, not weighted). On 11 Jun 2026 the neurologist noted slurring by late afternoon, which the engine scores as dysarthria. By then the arm and leg already had motor findings, so a reading decline would add a third region, not change the referral. For a limb-onset patient, expect the line to stay flat for months ([Eshghi 2022](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9489769/)). A flat line does not mean safe.

### 3. Body weight (whole body)

**Why.** Weight loss has strong ALS evidence ([Janse van Mantgem 2020](https://doi.org/10.1136/jnnp-2020-322909)). It is the one demo signal with years of stored history and a live API (Withings, the HealthKit export, Health Connect, or the Google Health API for Fitbit Aria scales). It is the top monitoring signal because it guides nutrition and gastrostomy timing ([EAN 2024](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11235832/)). It also shows Route 2: a device prompts the clinician, and the note finding carries the weight.

**What the screen shows.** A weekly weight line from mid-2024 to September 2026, anchored to the real note values. Add lines at 5% and 10% below his usual weight, the MDC band (2.4 kg), and a shaded Christmas window. When a decline is confirmed, show a banner: "Ask about weight loss and record it in the note." Show that the score moves only after the clinician records `weight_loss` (Mei weight 2.20).

**Real and simulated.** Real: four weights in the notes with their dates. They are 86 kg "two years ago" (as of 11 Jun 2026), "about 6 kg without trying since the summer" (16 Feb 2026), 78 kg (11 Jun 2026) and 77.5 kg (22 Sep 2026). Simulated: the weekly scale series between them, in real field names (Withings `getmeas` measure type 1, or HealthKit `bodyMass`), labelled simulated.

**Alex's timeline.** 86 kg to 78 kg is a 9.3% loss (computed by us), close to the 10% mark that went with a 23% higher risk of death ([Janse van Mantgem 2020](https://doi.org/10.1136/jnnp-2020-322909)). Suppose the 6 kg loss ran steadily from July 2025 to mid-February 2026, about 0.8 kg a month (our assumption). A weekly scale would then have passed the 2.4 kg MDC around October 2025, about 4 months before the February note (hypothetical, computed by us). That would not have changed his referral date, because the pattern rules had already fired in December 2024.

### 4. Timed walk gait speed (legs)

**Why.** It replaces stride-time variability, which has no published basis for its 1.0 MDC and an ICC under 0.20 in older adults ([Beauchet 2011](https://doi.org/10.1186/1743-0003-8-37)). Gait speed over a timed walk has an ALS MDC ([WatchALS](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13423962/)). The legs are Alex's second region, and spread is what fires `multi_region`. A stopwatch version needs no motion sensor, so the team could build it tonight. Alex also has a real clinic walk time.

## Signal by signal: what each measures, how noisy it is, and what ALS does to it

Each section covers what the signal measures; devices and APIs; access; sampling and how passive it is; region; noise or MDC, with source and population; the ALS evidence; how it feeds the tool; and pitfalls. Unless a section says otherwise, every ALS cohort was already diagnosed. For raw phone and watch motion access, see the other session's report, `reports/AirPods phone and watch gyro access.md`, sections "Watches relay through phones, and Fitbit closes November 28" and "A 60 Hz browser walk test is buildable but unvalidated for screening". We do not repeat it here.

### Hand and arm: tapping is built, typing separates mild cases best, wrist sensors have the strongest evidence

#### Phone finger tapping

**Measures.** How many times a finger taps a target in a fixed time. Our app uses one index finger for 10 s, each hand on its own. **Devices and APIs.** Any phone browser (our app); the open-source ResearchKit two-finger tapping task on iOS ([ResearchKit task header](https://github.com/ResearchKit/ResearchKit/blob/main/ResearchKitActiveTask/Common/ORKOrderedTask%2BORKPredefinedActiveTask.h)). No platform stores a hand measure ([Health Connect data types](https://developer.android.com/health-and-fitness/guides/health-connect/plan/data-types)). **Access.** None needed. **Sampling and region.** Active, weekly, about a minute; hand and arm.

**Noise or MDC.** WatchALS ran a 20-s alternating two-finger test weekly at home on iPhones for 24 weeks. ICC was 0.95 (dominant) and 0.96 (non-dominant) in 19 people with ALS. MDC95 was 35.5 and 32.6 in ALS, and 33.8 and 36 in 23 controls ([WatchALS](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13423962/)). No units are printed, and the column is headed "%MDC95". The same column gives speech in wpm and gait in m/s, so it is most likely taps per 20 s. Scaled to a 10-s test, that gives about 16 to 18 taps if noise scales with test length, or 23 to 25.5 if it scales with the square root (computed by us). If the "%" is literal, the MDC is about 33% to 36% of a person's own mean. Provisional value: a drop of about 18 taps per 10 s, or about 35% of baseline. In 12 healthy young adults, a 10-s single-finger iPhone test had ICC 0.729 (left) and 0.832 (right), and in 7 pilot volunteers the hands differed by about 8.4 taps ([Bohannon 2021](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8332647/)).

**ALS evidence (moderate).** People with ALS made fewer taps than controls with both hands, and taps correlated 0.71 to 0.74 with the self-entered ALSFRS-R fine-motor score. But scores rose over 24 weeks in both groups, a practice effect, and no net decline was shown ([WatchALS](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13423962/)). In nQiALS (63 ALS, 30 controls, own phones, 30-s alternating test), tapping separated ALS from controls only modestly (AUC 0.67, 49 against 22 analysed) and not at all in mild hand involvement (AUC 0.52) ([Acien 2024](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11263713/)).

**Feeds the tool.** `tap_right` and `tap_left` (region arm). Use the weekly mean of 2 or 3 trials (the app already averages same-day runs), an MDC of 18 taps, and two practice weeks before the baseline. **Pitfalls.** Practice gains hide decline: in an MS app, half the gain on a pinching task took 28 repetitions (abstract only) ([Woelfle 2021](https://doi.org/10.2196/30394)). Carpal tunnel syndrome lowers tapping too ([Seok 2019](https://doi.org/10.1002/mus.26424)). Hand dominance, phone model, fatigue and time of day add noise. Restart the baseline after hand surgery or a splint.

#### Digital pegboard

**Measures.** How fast a person moves virtual pegs into holes on the screen. **Devices and APIs.** WatchALS's grooved pegboard task on iPhone; ResearchKit's hole peg test ([ResearchKit task header](https://github.com/ResearchKit/ResearchKit/blob/main/ResearchKitActiveTask/Common/ORKOrderedTask%2BORKPredefinedActiveTask.h)). **Access.** Not built; ResearchKit is open source. **Sampling and region.** Active, weekly; hand and arm.

**Noise or MDC.** In ALS, ICC was 0.73 (dominant) and 0.81 (non-dominant); in controls 0.54 and 0.51. The MDC column read 12.9 in ALS for both hands, and 14.5 and 14 in controls, with no units stated ([WatchALS](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13423962/)). **ALS evidence (moderate).** It correlated 0.71 with the fine-motor score (dominant hand; 0.53 non-dominant, with a confidence interval crossing 0). It was the only fine-motor task that declined in ALS by the end of 24 weeks, in the non-dominant hand ([WatchALS](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13423962/)). One study of 19 people. **Feeds the tool.** Not now; a future second arm test. **Pitfalls.** Lower ICC than tapping, unclear MDC units, and large practice effects for dexterity tasks. Alex's real clinic nine-hole peg times on 22 Sep 2026 were 44 s (right) and 23 s (left).

#### Typing and keystroke timing

**Measures.** How long keys are held and the gaps between them while typing. Validated systems kept timing only, never text. **Devices and APIs.** Custom keyboards (the nQ keyboard in ALS, Neurokeys in MS) on iOS or Android; Apple SensorKit keyboard metrics (typing speed, hold and between-key times), which are research-only ([Apple: SRKeyboardMetrics](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/sensorkit/srkeyboardmetrics)); or an in-app copy-typing task. **Access.** A copy task needs no approval. Passive logging needs a custom keyboard or SensorKit's preapproved-research entitlement. **Sampling and region.** Active 5-minute copy task in nQiALS, or passive whenever the person types; hand and arm.

**Noise or MDC.** None in ALS. In MS, 14-day keystroke aggregates had test-retest ICC 0.601 to 0.965 and SEM 0.098 to 0.241 in z-score units ([Lam 2021](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8358561/)). Passive typing speed had ICC 0.714 over the first week (MS, abstract only) ([Chico-Garcia 2024](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.msard.2024.105595)).

**ALS evidence (moderate).** In nQiALS, people with ALS typed more slowly, with longer hold and flight times. A model on the copy task separated ALS from controls with AUC 0.89 (49 vs 22), and 0.93 in mild hand involvement (17 vs 22), where tapping scored 0.52. It correlated -0.44 with the fine-motor score. The passive progression model was weak: correlation with ALSFRS-R slope was about 0.2 ([Acien 2024](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11263713/)). Key hold time grew with progression in 19 people tracked with a finger accelerometer (abstract only) ([Londral 2015](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.clinph.2015.06.017)).

**Feeds the tool.** Not now. A future arm test (mean flight time, lower is better) once its weekly noise is measured. **Pitfalls.** One centre, cross-validated, no external test. Several authors work for nQ Medical, Area2 AI or Mitsubishi Tanabe. Phone model, screen size and autocorrect matter. Typing slows with age, and its age signal varies by time of day (abstract only) ([Moon 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12709153/)).

#### Wrist arm movement

**Measures.** Counts and speed of forearm bending and turning from a wrist accelerometer in daily life, or the intensity of a 45-second arm-raise exercise. **Devices and APIs.** Every ALS study used research ActiGraph watches. A consumer version needs raw accelerometer data: a custom Apple Watch app, Wear OS `SensorManager`, the Samsung Health Sensor SDK (25 Hz), or Garmin Connect IQ ([Samsung Health Sensor SDK](https://developer.samsung.com/health/sensor/overview.html)). No aggregated API offers it. **Access.** A custom watch app; Samsung needs a partnership to distribute. **Sampling and region.** Recommended as a short active task three times a week; passive all-day wear also works. Hand and arm.

**Noise or MDC.** No MDC published. Arm-raise metrics had test-retest ICC 0.80 to 0.96 across week 1 in the 257 to 259 of 329 people with ALS who did all three sessions ([Straczkiewicz 2026](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12825179/)). A wrist composite score had ICC 0.84 to 0.90 ([Gupta 2023](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10442344/)).

**ALS evidence (strong).** Forearm movement counts fell 4.4% to 4.9% a month in 20 people at MGH (computed by us from the table) ([Straczkiewicz 2024, eBioMedicine](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10914560/)). They fell 2.56% to 2.74% a month in 202 people, faster in relative terms than the ALSFRS-R fine-motor score at 2.10% ([Straczkiewicz 2024, JNER](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11662782/)). Arm-raise intensity fell and was linked to fine-motor scores (model R² 0.875 to 0.879 in all 329, and 0.897 to 0.899 in the 193 with full-day wear) ([Straczkiewicz 2026](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12825179/)). A wrist composite correlated 0.65 to 0.72 with the ALSFRS-R total and fine-motor scores in 376 people ([Gupta 2023](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10442344/)). People shift use to the unaffected arm: the dominant-to-non-dominant log ratio was +0.41 with non-dominant onset and -0.27 with dominant onset ([Straczkiewicz 2024, JNER](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11662782/)).

**Feeds the tool.** Not now. A future arm test (weekly median arm-raise intensity) once a consumer watch port and its noise are checked. **Pitfalls.** All-day wear is hard to keep up: on average only 51.3 of 282.8 collected days were valid ([Straczkiewicz 2024, JNER](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11662782/)). In the arm-raise study, only 193 people met the free-living wear rule, 41.5% fewer than for the exercise ([Straczkiewicz 2026](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12825179/)). Over 8 weeks, a 5% to 10% change is likely (computed by us) and no MDC exists. Consumer watches are unvalidated for these metrics.

#### Home grip strength

**Measures.** Squeeze force on a handheld dynamometer. Phones have no force sensor. **Devices and APIs.** A home dynamometer; no platform API checked stores grip. **Access.** Buy a device. **Sampling and region.** Active; daily for 3 months, then twice a week in the study below. Hand and arm. **Noise or MDC.** None; only slope SDs were published (0.041 to 0.065 per day, units not stated).

**ALS evidence (moderate).** In 61 people measuring at home, daily readings cut the estimated trial size for the right hand from 383 to 101 per arm, against monthly readings; for the left hand the cut was small (136 to 114) ([Rutkove 2020](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7359124/)). In UK Biobank, grip below 21 kg went with a higher later ALS risk, a population finding, not an individual test (abstract only) ([Peters 2025](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-025-89080-3)). **Feeds the tool.** Not fed. **Pitfalls.** Many unexplained missing values. Alex's real clinic grip on 22 Sep 2026 was 14 kg (right) and 32 kg (left).

### Legs: gait speed over a measured course beats every passive leg metric

#### Timed walk gait speed

**Measures.** Distance divided by time over a measured course at usual pace. **Devices and APIs.** Any phone as a stopwatch (a phone at the waist adds steps and cadence); ResearchKit's timed walk task ([ResearchKit task header](https://github.com/ResearchKit/ResearchKit/blob/main/ResearchKitActiveTask/Common/ORKOrderedTask%2BORKPredefinedActiveTask.h)). **Access.** None. **Sampling and region.** Active, weekly, 2 or 3 walks; legs.

**Noise or MDC.** WatchALS's 60-second walk had ICC 0.93 in ALS and 0.68 in controls, and MDC95 0.179 m/s in ALS and 0.284 m/s in controls ([WatchALS](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13423962/)). Its "%MDC95" header conflicts with these m/s values, so treat them as approximate. In 93 adults with neuromuscular disease, the 2-minute walk MDC was 13.7 m and the 6-minute walk MDC 38.8 m (abstract only) ([Knak 2017](https://doi.org/10.2340/16501977-2222)).

**ALS evidence (moderate).** Gait speed correlated 0.83 with the ALSFRS gross-motor score, and combined task features separated ALS from controls with AUC 0.75 to 0.92 ([WatchALS](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13423962/)). In 9 people walking twice a week at home with a shoe sensor, speed fell 0.021 m/s a month; the 4 who later needed a walking aid fell 0.135 m/s a month before the switch ([Lukac](https://doi.org/10.1159/000530067)). In clinic, 186 people with ALS who could walk, with or without an aid, reached 66% of their predicted 6-minute distance (abstract only) ([Sanjak 2017](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.apmr.2017.04.004)). Timed Up and Go time rose steadily over 6 months in 31 people (abstract only) ([Montes 2007](https://doi.org/10.1080/17482960701435931)).

**Feeds the tool.** Replace `walk`: unit m/s, `higherIsBetter` true, region leg, weekly mean of 2 or 3 walks. MDC 0.28 m/s before diagnosis (controls-level noise) and 0.18 m/s after (our recommendation). WatchALS timed a 60-s back-and-forth walk, not a 10-m stopwatch walk, so treat its MDC as a starting point and check it against the team's own repeat walks. **Pitfalls.** At 0.021 m/s a month, an average decliner needs about 8.6 months to pass the MDC; a fast decliner about 1.3 months (computed by us). Walking aids and ankle braces change speed, so restart the baseline after one. Many WatchALS participants could not finish the walk or changed aids. Home speed ran 0.19 m/s slower than clinic speed ([Lukac](https://doi.org/10.1159/000530067)).

#### Stride-time variability

**Measures.** How much stride time varies (coefficient of variation) during a walk. This is today's `walk` test. **Devices and APIs.** A phone motion sensor in the browser at the waist (see the AirPods report for the build). **Access.** Motion permission over HTTPS. **Sampling and region.** Active; legs.

**Noise or MDC.** No ALS MDC, so the 1.0 placeholder has no published basis. In healthy older adults and people with frontotemporal dementia, stride-time variability had ICC under 0.20 in most conditions, against 0.96 for mean stride time (abstract only) ([Beauchet 2011](https://doi.org/10.1186/1743-0003-8-37)). Phone estimates of step variability showed poor validity and reliability ([Rashid 2021](https://doi.org/10.3390/s22010124)). On foot-sensor data, 10 strides gave estimates 26% too low, and about 50 strides were needed (computed by us) ([PhysioNet gaitndd analysis](https://physionet.org/content/gaitndd/1.0.0/)).

**ALS evidence (moderate).** 42 ambulatory people with ALS had higher stance- and swing-time variability than 16 controls on a wearable gait device ([Dubbioso 2023](https://doi.org/10.1111/ene.15936)). Median variability was 4.86% in ALS against 2.53% in controls on public foot-sensor data, in groups that were not age-matched (computed by us) ([PhysioNet gaitndd analysis](https://physionet.org/content/gaitndd/1.0.0/)). In 182 people with an ankle sensor, stride-duration variability correlated -0.42 with gross motor and changed less than steps (preprint, not peer reviewed) ([Burke 2026 preprint](https://www.medrxiv.org/content/10.64898/2026.05.27.26354200v1.full)). **Feeds the tool.** Move it to an exploratory output, reported only with at least about 50 clean strides; stop feeding it. **Pitfalls.** It needs 60 to 90 seconds of straight walking, a fixed phone position, and no change of walking aid.

#### iPhone walking speed

**Measures.** Average speed during steady walking on flat ground, written by the iPhone 10 to 30 times on a typical day ([Apple: walkingSpeed](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/healthkit/hkquantitytypeidentifier/walkingspeed)). **Devices and APIs.** iPhone 8 or later, carried near the waist, with height set; HealthKit `walkingSpeed`. Android has no passive equivalent; Health Connect's `SpeedRecord` comes mostly from workouts (an inference in the notes). **Access.** HealthKit read permission, or the Health app export with no app at all. **Sampling and region.** Passive; none in wheelchair mode; legs. Over 80% of users with any walking bout get at least five estimates a day ([Apple mobility white paper](https://www.apple.com/healthcare/docs/site/Measuring_Walking_Quality_Through_iPhone_Mobility_Metrics.pdf)).

**Noise or MDC.** Apple's MDC within one lab visit in older adults was 0.08, 0.14 and 0.23 m/s at the 10th, 50th and 90th percentiles ([Apple mobility white paper](https://www.apple.com/healthcare/docs/site/Measuring_Walking_Quality_Through_iPhone_Mobility_Metrics.pdf)). Retested after a week during a supervised 6-minute walk, MDC95 was 0.20 m/s in 24 healthy seniors and 0.22 m/s in 20 adults with usable retests (28 of each were tested) ([Sci Rep 2023](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10067003/)). No one has published day-to-day or week-to-week noise, so the noise of a weekly median is unknown.

**ALS evidence (weak).** No ALS study used it. Gait speed itself falls in ALS ([Lukac](https://doi.org/10.1159/000530067); timed walk above), but this passive consumer version is untested. Closest evidence outside ALS: after lower-limb fractures, iPhone walking speed fell 0.200 m/s from injury to 8 weeks and recovered 0.071 m/s from 12 to 26 weeks (abstract only) ([JBJS 2025](https://doi.org/10.2106/JBJS.24.01305)). **Feeds the tool.** Context only for now. Later, perhaps a leg test on the weekly median with a person-specific MDC estimated from the weeks before the visit. **Pitfalls.** It needs the phone in a pocket near the hip; 21.1% of retests had no gait data, possibly because of loose trousers ([Sci Rep 2023](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10067003/)). It is iPhone-only. Its value is history: owners may already have data from before symptoms ([Apple WWDC20](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2020/10656/)).

#### iPhone gait quality metrics

**Measures.** Step length, double support (the share of a walk with both feet down), asymmetry, and Walking Steadiness, a weekly fall-risk score with OK, Low and Very Low classes. **Devices and APIs.** iPhone 8 or later through HealthKit; Walking Steadiness is read-only ([Apple: appleWalkingSteadiness](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/healthkit/hkquantitytypeidentifier/applewalkingsteadiness)). **Access.** HealthKit read or the Health export. **Sampling and region.** Passive; Walking Steadiness weekly, with an alert about a month after a person drops into Low ([Apple WWDC21](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2021/10287/)); legs.

**Noise or MDC.** Step length MDC95 was 6.1 cm in seniors and 9.4 cm in adults; double support 1.8% to 3.4% ([Sci Rep 2023](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10067003/)). Apple reports double support ICC 0.53, and, because few participants walked asymmetrically, induced asymmetry with a knee brace to test it ([Apple mobility white paper](https://www.apple.com/healthcare/docs/site/Measuring_Walking_Quality_Through_iPhone_Mobility_Metrics.pdf)). We found no published Walking Steadiness validation. **ALS evidence (weak).** None for these metrics. Indirectly, gait variability is higher in ALS on research sensors ([Dubbioso 2023](https://doi.org/10.1111/ene.15936)). **Feeds the tool.** Context only. **Pitfalls.** Apple says knee-brace asymmetry may differ from asymmetry due to neurodegenerative disease. Walking Steadiness lags by weeks.

#### Watch six-minute walk estimate

**Measures.** Apple Watch's weekly estimate of how far the wearer would walk in a clinic 6-minute walk test (capped at 500 m), built from the previous 4 weeks. **Devices and APIs.** Apple Watch Series 3 or later; HealthKit `sixMinuteWalkTestDistance`. **Access.** HealthKit read; a recalibration call after injury needs an entitlement. **Sampling and region.** Passive, weekly; it needs at least 3 days with more than 8 hours of wear in the past week, and 10 such days in 4 weeks ([Apple six-minute walk white paper](https://www.apple.com/healthcare/docs/site/Using_Apple_Watch_to_Estimate_Six_Minute_Walk_Distance.pdf)). Legs.

**Noise or MDC.** In Apple's validation in older adults, the median person's week-to-week SD was 17 m (90th percentile 35 m). That gives an MDC95 of about 47 m for the median person and 97 m at the 90th percentile (computed by us). Error was 1 plus or minus 51 m, with ICC 0.913 ([Apple six-minute walk white paper](https://www.apple.com/healthcare/docs/site/Using_Apple_Watch_to_Estimate_Six_Minute_Walk_Distance.pdf)). **ALS evidence (weak).** No ALS study used the estimate. The clinic test it imitates is reduced in ALS, at 66% of predicted, and correlated 0.71 with gross motor (abstract only) ([Sanjak 2017](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.apmr.2017.04.004)). **Feeds the tool.** Context only. **Pitfalls.** Its 4-week window blurs fast change, and rebuilding history after a recalibration can take up to 14 days ([Apple WWDC21](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2021/10287/)). It is noisier in walker users. The validation cohort was adults 65 and over in one California region.

#### Stair speed

**Measures.** Speed up and down a flight of stairs from an Apple Watch Series 5 or later, about 20 samples a day; also flights climbed ([Apple: stairAscentSpeed](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/healthkit/hkquantitytypeidentifier/stairascentspeed)). **Devices and APIs.** HealthKit; Health Connect `FloorsClimbedRecord`; the Google Health API `floors` type. **Access.** Per-type read. **Sampling and region.** Passive; legs. **Noise or MDC.** None published; Apple gives no accuracy data. **ALS evidence (weak).** No ALS study measured stair speed. Indirectly, accelerometry during short limb exercises in 350 people (tested on 83 held out) predicted the ALSFRS-R stair-climbing item with AUC 0.701 ([Vieira 2022](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8993812/)). **Feeds the tool.** Context only. **Pitfalls.** It depends on whether the person uses stairs at all, and stops in wheelchair mode.

#### Falls and fall detection

**Measures.** Falls an Apple Watch detects that the wearer confirms, or that escalate to emergency services. Dismissed alerts are not recorded ([Apple: numberOfTimesFallen](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/healthkit/hkquantitytypeidentifier/numberoftimesfallen)). **Devices and APIs.** Apple Watch SE, Series 4 or later, and Ultra; HealthKit. Real-time events through `CMFallDetectionManager` need an Apple entitlement ([Apple: CMFallDetectionManager](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/coremotion/cmfalldetectionmanager)). **Access.** HealthKit read. **Sampling and region.** Passive, event-driven. Fall Detection turns on automatically from age 55 ([Apple Support: Fall Detection](https://support.apple.com/en-us/108896)). From 18 to 55, a new watch turns it on only during workouts ([Apple Watch User Guide: Fall Detection](https://support.apple.com/guide/watch/manage-fall-detection-apd34c409704/watchos)). Legs.

**Noise or MDC.** Events, so no MDC. Apple publishes no accuracy data. Thirteen published algorithms caught 57.0% plus or minus 27.3% of 29 real falls (best 82.8%) (abstract only) ([Bagala 2012](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0037062)); a meta-analysis found 81.9% sensitivity for falls in daily life, from 3 fall studies (abstract only) ([Singh 2024, fall detection](https://doi.org/10.2196/56972)); an Apple Watch Series 5 caught 14 of 300 wheelchair falls (abstract only) ([Abou 2021](https://doi.org/10.1080/10400435.2021.1923087)).

**ALS evidence (weak for the device; falls themselves are well documented).** Among 66 people with ALS, 47% of walkers fell within 3 months, and 59% reported falls at the 6-month follow-up (abstract only) ([Alencar 2026](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.bjpt.2026.101623)). In UK Biobank, 11.3% of ALS cases had a recorded fall in the 5 years before diagnosis, against 3.2% of matched Alzheimer's cases; the denominators are unclear ([Pang 2025](https://doi.org/10.1002/jcsm.70003)). Among 2,092 Canadians with ALS in home care, nursing homes and complex continuing care, 44.0% had falls (abstract only) ([Kehyayan 2014](https://doi.org/10.1017/s0317167100016656)). No study tested watch fall detection in ALS. **Feeds the tool.** Not a home test. A recorded fall prompts the clinician to ask and record `falls` in a note (Mei weight 2.70, region leg, not a motor sign). Better still, ask weekly about falls and near-falls. **Pitfalls.** Detection misses about 18% to 43% of real falls (computed by us from those sensitivities). At 45, Alex would have had it on by default only during workouts, on a newly set-up watch.

### Whole body: weight is the strongest passive signal; the rest is context

#### Daily steps and activity

**Measures.** Daily steps, active and sedentary time, and "best effort" measures such as peak 6-minute activity or peak cadence. **Devices and APIs.** HealthKit `stepCount` from iPhone and Apple Watch ([Apple: stepCount](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/healthkit/hkquantitytypeidentifier/stepcount)); Health Connect `StepsRecord`, which an Android 14 phone (SDK extension 20 or later) fills by itself once an app holds `READ_STEPS` ([Health Connect: read data](https://developer.android.com/health-and-fitness/guides/health-connect/develop/read-data)); the Google Health API `steps` type at 1-minute resolution ([Google Health API data types](https://developers.google.com/health/data-types)); Garmin and Withings APIs. **Access.** Per-type permission; free tiers suffice for a demo. **Sampling and region.** Passive; reduce to a weekly median; whole body.

**Noise or MDC.** For a Garmin watch in 104 people with Parkinson's disease over 5 days, ICC was 0.89 and MDC 1580 steps a day, 27% of the mean (abstract only) ([Bianchini 2025](https://doi.org/10.2196/63153)). For duty-cycled phone steps in ALS, the day-level residual SD was 1.914 log units ([Karas 2024](https://doi.org/10.1002/acn3.52050)). A weekly mean would then need about a 7-fold change to pass its MDC (computed by us).

**ALS evidence (strong).** Of 40 people enrolled in Calgary, a Fitbit Sense counted about 14% fewer steps than a research wrist sensor (34 people), and both showed a similar decline in the 18 with 6 months or more of paired data ([Jewett 2026](https://doi.org/10.1002/mus.70323)). Phone step counts fell about 8.6% a month in 45 people (computed by us) ([Karas 2024](https://doi.org/10.1002/acn3.52050)). Wrist activity fell 0.03 to 0.07 SD a month, and peak 6-minute activity needed fewer trial participants than the ALSFRS-R for trials longer than 12 months ([Holdom 2025](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ebiom.2025.105779)). Hip-worn active time fell about 2.3% a month in 42 people (computed by us) ([van Eijk 2019](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00415-019-09427-5)). Ankle steps fell faster in relative terms than the self-entered ALSFRS-R in 90% of 182 people (preprint) ([Burke 2026 preprint](https://www.medrxiv.org/content/10.64898/2026.05.27.26354200v1.full)). Before diagnosis there is only a population link: in UK Biobank, each SD more wrist activity went with lower later ALS risk (HR 0.75, 98 cases), which looks like a risk marker, not an early sign ([Gao 2026](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13691708/)).

**Feeds the tool.** Context only, region general; never a motor-region decline. **Pitfalls.** Summer brought 394.5 more daily steps than winter in 543 Fitbit users in a depression study (abstract only) ([Zhang 2024](https://doi.org/10.2196/55302)). In daily life, iPhone steps were 21.5% off, probably because the phone was not always carried (abstract only) ([Duncan 2018](https://doi.org/10.1080/02640414.2017.1409855)). Wrist trackers miss nearly all steps with a rollator ([Copenhagen rollator study 2020](https://doi.org/10.1186/s11556-019-0235-0)), and firmware updates move counts.

#### GPS life-space

**Measures.** Time at home and distance travelled, from phone location. **Devices and APIs.** Phone GPS in a research app such as Beiwe. No consumer health platform stores it as a metric. **Access.** Location permission in a custom app. **Sampling and region.** Passive (Beiwe sampled 1 minute on, 10 off); whole body. **Noise or MDC.** None published.

**ALS evidence (weak).** In 45 people over about 10 months, home time (21.25 hours a day at baseline) and distance did not change significantly, though both were associated with the self-entered ALSFRS-R across visits ([Karas 2024](https://doi.org/10.1002/acn3.52050)). During COVID-19 restrictions, home time rose from 19.4 to 23.7 hours a day in 8 people (abstract only) ([Beukenhorst 2020](https://doi.org/10.1002/mus.27110)). **Feeds the tool.** Context only. **Pitfalls.** Lockdowns, weather and life changes dominate. Android phones had more GPS gaps than iPhones in pooled phone studies ([Karas 2024](https://doi.org/10.1002/acn3.52050)). Location is sensitive data.

#### Body weight

**Measures.** Body mass at each weigh-in. **Devices and APIs.** Withings scales (measure type 1, webhook category 1) ([Withings OpenAPI spec](https://developer.withings.com/openapi.yaml)); Fitbit Aria scales through the Google Health API `weight` type; the Garmin Index scale; any scale or manual entry written to HealthKit `bodyMass` ([Apple: bodyMass](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/healthkit/hkquantitytypeidentifier/bodymass)) or Health Connect `WeightRecord`. Keep manual entries separate. **Access.** Withings' free plan covers up to 10 users, but accounts created after 12 Oct 2026 need Withings+ to share ([Withings plans](https://static.withings.com/content/api/plans/plans.html)). **Sampling and region.** Semi-passive (the person steps on the scale); use a weekly mean of at least 3 morning weighings; whole body.

**Noise or MDC.** In 46 free-living adults, on average weight-stable, weighed fasting two weeks apart, the SD of the change was 1.2 kg, giving an MDC95 of about 2.35 kg (2.16 and 2.55 kg in the two cohorts; computed by us) ([Bhutani 2017](https://doi.org/10.14814/phy2.13336)). Weight swings about 0.35% within the week, heaviest Sunday to Tuesday ([Turicchi 2020](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0232152)). In 10 older inpatients, weights taken at the same time each day differed by 0.4 kg or less (abstract only) ([Vivanti 2013](https://doi.org/10.1111/jhn.12034)). Smart scales had a median weight error of 0 to 0.3 kg (abstract only) ([Frija-Masson 2021](https://doi.org/10.2196/22487)).

**ALS evidence (strong).** In 2,420 Dutch patients, 67.5% had lost weight by diagnosis (mean 5.5% in spinal onset, 6.9% in bulbar onset), including 35.1% of those without swallowing problems. Each extra 10% lost raised the risk of death by 23% (abstract only) ([Janse van Mantgem 2020](https://doi.org/10.1136/jnnp-2020-322909)). From recalled weights, BMI fell sharply about 10 years before onset (393 cases) ([Peter 2017](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10654-017-0318-z)) and fell in the 5 years before diagnosis ([Goutman 2022](https://doi.org/10.1002/mus.27744)) (both abstract only). A 5% loss from usual weight at diagnosis raised the risk of death 30% ([Marin 2011](https://doi.org/10.1136/jnnp.2010.211474)), and the monthly rate of loss predicted survival in 620 people ([Moglia 2019](https://doi.org/10.1136/jnnp-2018-319611)) (both abstract only). No study tracked home-scale weights before diagnosis.

**Feeds the tool.** `weight` (region general) with an MDC of 2.4 kg. A confirmed loss prompts the clinician to ask and record `weight_loss` (Mei weight 2.20). Today a weight-only decline also escalates an unclear case through `unclear_with_decline`; see the MDC section. **Pitfalls.** Christmas added about 1.1 kg on average, and 0.35% was still there in March ([Turicchi 2020](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0232152)). Time of day, clothing and hydration matter. A loss of about 0.64 kg a month, the median before the first visit in [Shimizu 2012](https://doi.org/10.3109/17482968.2012.678366) at a height of 1.75 m, takes about 3.7 months to pass the MDC (computed by us), so also test the slope over the whole series and the 5% cumulative mark.

#### Body composition

**Measures.** Fat-free mass, muscle and fat from bioimpedance (BIA) on foot-to-foot scales or a watch. **Devices and APIs.** Withings scales (measure types 5, 6, 8 and 76, plus segmental types 173 to 175) ([Withings OpenAPI spec](https://developer.withings.com/openapi.yaml)); Galaxy Watch4 or later BIA through the Samsung Health Sensor SDK ([Samsung Health Sensor SDK](https://developer.samsung.com/health/sensor/overview.html)); Fitbit Aria body fat. No brand checked writes lean mass to Health Connect. **Access.** As for weight; watch BIA needs a Samsung partnership to distribute. **Sampling and region.** Each weigh-in, or a spot reading on the watch; whole body.

**Noise or MDC.** Smartwatch BIA had a fat-free-mass precision RMSE of 0.7 kg in 75 multiethnic adults, so a change needs about 1.9 kg to exceed noise (computed by us; abstract only) ([Bennett 2022](https://doi.org/10.1093/ajcn/nqac200)). Fifteen BIA devices had test-retest precision errors of 0.0% to 0.49% body fat in healthy adults (abstract only) ([Siedler 2022](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0007114522003749)).

**ALS evidence (weak).** With research-grade BIA, a low phase angle predicted poorer survival in 168 people ([Desport 2008](https://doi.org/10.1080/17482960801925039)), and falls in phase angle and fat-free mass predicted shorter survival whatever weight did ([Roubeau 2015](https://doi.org/10.1002/mus.24419)) (both abstract only). Consumer devices are unvalidated in ALS and inaccurate: three smart scales had median fat-mass errors of -2.2, -4.4 and -3.7 kg against DXA ([Frija-Masson 2021](https://doi.org/10.2196/22487)), and Galaxy Watch5 skeletal muscle agreed weakly with DXA (CCC 0.45) ([Carrier 2025](https://doi.org/10.3389/fspor.2025.1644082)) (both abstract only). **Feeds the tool.** Context only. **Pitfalls.** The equations were built on healthy people, and ALS muscle wasting may bias them further; one group needed an ALS-specific equation (abstract only) ([Tandan 2022](https://doi.org/10.1093/ajcn/nqac016)).

#### Resting heart rate and HRV

**Measures.** Resting heart rate and beat-to-beat variability (SDNN on Apple Watch; RMSSD on Android, Fitbit, Oura and WHOOP). **Devices and APIs.** HealthKit `restingHeartRate` and HRV types ([Apple: restingHeartRate](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/healthkit/hkquantitytypeidentifier/restingheartrate)); Health Connect `RestingHeartRateRecord` and `HeartRateVariabilityRmssdRecord`; Google Health API daily types; Oura, WHOOP, Garmin and Withings. **Access.** Per platform. **Sampling and region.** Passive, daily or nightly; whole body.

**Noise or MDC.** Across 92,457 Fitbit users, the within-person SD of daily resting heart rate was 3.03 bpm. That gives an MDC95 of about 8.4 bpm for a single day (computed by us). Season moved the population mean by 2 bpm ([Quer 2020](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0227709)).

**ALS evidence (moderate).** With a consumer chest strap, 68 people with ALS had lower HRV and a higher average heart rate than 56 healthy people (abstract only) ([Maset-Roig 2024](https://doi.org/10.3390/s24072355)). In 102 newly diagnosed people, HRV was lower than in controls and fell further over 12 months (abstract only) ([Dubbioso 2023, autonomic](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00415-023-11832-w)). Lower SDNN at diagnosis predicted poorer survival (abstract only) ([Li 2026, Holter](https://doi.org/10.1177/22143602261472982)). No wrist or ring HRV study exists in ALS, and no study shows HRV change before diagnosis. **Feeds the tool.** Context only. After diagnosis, a sustained rise in resting heart rate could prompt a check for infection or hypoventilation. **Pitfalls.** Age, fitness, anxiety, alcohol, infection and drugs all move HRV. Watch N-N intervals had an error of 31.31% at rest (abstract only) ([Sensors 2025](https://doi.org/10.3390/s25082380)). In a small home study, 3 of 4 controls also had very low pNN50 ([Brown 2022](https://doi.org/10.3389/frai.2022.910049)).

#### Sleep tracking

**Measures.** Sleep duration, stages, time awake and wake-ups. **Devices and APIs.** Apple Watch sleep stages; Health Connect `SleepSessionRecord`; Google Health API `sleep`; Withings Sleep (`wakeupcount`, `out_of_bed_count`); Oura, Samsung and Garmin. **Access.** Per platform. **Sampling and region.** Passive, nightly; whole body. **Noise or MDC.** No test-retest data. Against a sleep lab in 35 healthy adults, deep-sleep ICC was 0.13 to 0.36 and REM 0.13 to 0.37, and the Apple Watch recorded nothing for 6 people ([Sensors 2024, sleep trackers](https://doi.org/10.3390/s24206532)).

**ALS evidence (weak).** By sleep-lab or questionnaire, about half of people with ALS have a sleep disorder (31 studies, 2,762 people, abstract only) ([Xie 2026](https://doi.org/10.3389/fneur.2026.1889217)). Their sleep is shorter and more broken, with less REM (abstract only) ([Congiu 2019](https://doi.org/10.1093/sleep/zsz164)). Presymptomatic gene carriers show EEG changes in sleep (abstract only) ([Lang 2025](https://doi.org/10.1172/JCI194555)) that worsened over about 2 years (abstract only) ([Lang 2026](https://doi.org/10.1111/ene.70755)), but consumer trackers cannot measure them. No wearable sleep study exists in ALS. **Feeds the tool.** Context only. **Pitfalls.** Depression, night-time urination, cramps, pain and sleeping pills; NIV changes the signal.

#### Wrist temperature

**Measures.** Nightly wrist skin temperature against a personal baseline. **Devices and APIs.** Apple Watch Series 8 or later (`appleSleepingWristTemperature`, read-only) ([Apple: appleSleepingWristTemperature](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/healthkit/hkquantitytypeidentifier/applesleepingwristtemperature)); Health Connect `SkinTemperatureRecord`; Google Health API skin temperature; Withings, Oura and WHOOP. **Access.** Per platform. **Sampling and region.** Passive, nightly; whole body. **Noise or MDC.** None published; a living review of Apple Watch accuracy found wrist temperature not yet validated ([npj Digit Med 2026 living review](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41746-025-02238-1)).

**ALS evidence (not studied).** No study has tested wrist or skin temperature as a sign of ALS in people. A review notes disturbed thermoregulation in ALS animal models and calls for human studies (abstract only) ([Dupuis 2018](https://doi.org/10.1016/B978-0-444-64074-1.00046-X)). Closest use outside ALS: Apple lists exercise, jet lag, menstrual cycles and illness as causes of change ([Apple: appleSleepingWristTemperature](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/healthkit/hkquantitytypeidentifier/applesleepingwristtemperature)). **Feeds the tool.** Context only, as a flag for possible fever and for cold nights when SpO2 may be unreliable; the FDA lists skin temperature among oximeter confounders ([FDA draft guidance](https://www.fda.gov/media/184896/download)).

### Speech and swallowing: reading aloud is strong; swallowing has only a questionnaire

#### Reading rate

**Measures.** Words per minute reading a fixed passage aloud. Our app times a fixed passage of about 100 words with Start and Stop buttons. **Devices and APIs.** Any phone microphone; WatchALS scored the standard Bamboo passage with automatic speech recognition. No platform stores speech measures ([Google Health API data types](https://developers.google.com/health/data-types)). **Access.** None. **Sampling and region.** Active, weekly, about a minute; speech and swallowing.

**Noise or MDC.** On iPhones at home, weekly for 24 weeks, ICC was 0.97 in ALS and 0.79 in controls; MDC95 was 22.1 wpm in ALS and 31.3 wpm in controls ([WatchALS](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13423962/)). The table header says "%MDC95", but the text gives words per minute. Lab estimates are larger: 36.57 wpm ([Stipancic 2018](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6693567/)) and 42.60 wpm over 3 to 6 months in controls ([Barnett 2020](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7080316/)).

**ALS evidence (strong).** In 166 people tested in labs, speaking rate fell 1.74 wpm a month in spinal onset and 4.16 in bulbar onset ([Eshghi 2022](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9489769/)). In 129 people reading remotely, rate fell 0.0344 wpm a week in non-bulbar and 0.2373 in bulbar onset. People whose speech item stayed at 3 still read at 136 wpm against 174 in controls ([Kothare 2023](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11246071/)). On home phones, 11 people with normal bulbar scores spoke 0.77 SD slower than 21 controls ([Stegmann 2020](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7555482/)). With bulbar subscores of 11 or 12, passage rate was already lower than in controls, 162 against 184 wpm (lab) ([Barnett 2020](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7080316/)). Speaking rate falls before intelligibility does ([Green 2013](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3833808/)).

**Feeds the tool.** `reading` (region bulbar), weekly mean of same-day readings at the same time of day. MDC 31 wpm before diagnosis and 22 after. A decline counts as a bulbar motor region. **Pitfalls.** Limb-onset change over 8 weeks is about 0.3 to 3.2 wpm (computed by us). Practice raises scores: controls gained 0.25 wpm a week ([Kothare 2023](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11246071/)). Time of day matters (Alex: 165 against 118 wpm). Reading ability, vision, cognition, accent, colds and room noise all add error. Our passage and button timing differ from WatchALS's.

#### Speech timing and articulation

**Measures.** From the same reading, or a fast "pa-ta-ka": percent pause time, pause count, speaking duration, articulation rate, timing alignment (CTA, from Modality.AI) and articulatory precision (AP, from Aural Analytics). **Devices and APIs.** Phone microphone. Apple SensorKit speech metrics give words per minute and pauses from Siri and phone calls, without raw audio, for approved research only ([Apple: telephonySpeechMetrics](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/sensorkit/srsensor/telephonyspeechmetrics)). **Access.** Percent pause needs only an on-device pause detector; CTA and AP are proprietary. **Sampling and region.** Active (no extra task); speech and swallowing.

**Noise or MDC.** AP had an MDC of 0.85 on its 0 to 10 scale, measured over days 15 to 45 on home phones in studies of 110 people with ALS and 26 healthy people ([Stegmann 2024](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11496019/)). Percent pause had ICC 0.49 and MDC95 9.39 points in 46 controls retested 3 to 6 months apart in labs and clinics ([Barnett 2020](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7080316/)). Modality features had ICC 0.54 to 0.97 across two run-in sessions ([Neumann 2026](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13324186/)).

**ALS evidence (strong).** Percent pause separated people with normal bulbar scores from controls (AUC 0.83, against 0.65 for rate) ([Allison 2017](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5530595/)). CTA was the most responsive measure, reaching its MCID in 3.86 weeks in bulbar onset and 8.32 in non-bulbar onset ([Kothare 2023](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11246071/)). With the speech item steady at 3, speaking duration and CTA still worsened faster in 47 people than in controls ([Neumann 2024](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11357899/)). AP and speaking rate caught bulbar change before the ALSFRS-R bulbar subscale did ([Stegmann 2020](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7555482/)). On home phones, percent pause correlated -0.62 with slow vital capacity in 36 people (abstract only) ([Connaghan 2025](https://doi.org/10.1002/mus.28435)).

**Feeds the tool.** Context beside reading rate until its home noise is measured. After that, it could become the bulbar test's second output, but only one bulbar test should escalate, or false alarms double. **Pitfalls.** Most authors of the Modality papers work for Modality.AI; AP compares speech with American English; cognitive impairment changes pauses ([Barnett 2020](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7080316/)).

#### Voice acoustic features

**Measures.** Jitter, shimmer, harmonics-to-noise ratio and pitch variability on sustained vowels and syllable repetition. **Devices and APIs.** Phone microphone. The VOC-ALS dataset holds one clinic session per person, recorded on a Samsung Galaxy S8+ at 8 kHz ([Dubbioso 2024, VOC-ALS](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11271596/)). **Access.** VOC-ALS needs a Synapse account, and the team README says its data may not be redistributed. **Sampling and region.** Active; speech and swallowing.

**Noise or MDC.** In a mobile app, median ICC was 0.31 for sustained-vowel features, 0.55 for sentence reading and 0.02 for connected speech (the last only in people with ALS and suspected FTD); only 11% of features exceeded 0.75 ([Stegmann 2020, repeatability](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7772887/)). Phone jitter and shimmer error was "problematic" ([Jannetts 2019](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30779425/)), and HNR and shimmer were biased ([Fahed 2022](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36379826/)) (both abstract only; the two disagree on jitter).

**ALS evidence (moderate).** In 102 people with ALS and 51 controls, 10 features differed across groups, mostly pitch variability and jitter. Non-dysarthric patients did not differ from controls on the strongest "pa" feature ([Dubbioso 2024, VOC-ALS](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11271596/)). A meta-analysis found jitter, shimmer and noise-to-harmonics ratio separated bulbar ALS from controls (abstract only) ([Chiaramonte 2019](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31760837/)). **Feeds the tool.** Research only. **Pitfalls.** Some phones' signal processing distorts jitter and HNR in running speech (abstract only) ([Lebacq 2017](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28320627/)); sex and language matter.

#### Tongue movement video

**Measures.** How fast the tongue moves side to side, from a phone video. **Devices and APIs.** Phone camera. **Access.** A research method; nothing to install. **Sampling and region.** Active; speech and swallowing. **Noise or MDC.** None published. **ALS evidence (moderate).** In 37 people with ALS and 20 controls, movement frequency was 0.14 against 0.40 (p under 0.001), linked to dysarthria but not to swallowing trouble (abstract only) ([Rocha 2026](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41829459/)). **Feeds the tool.** Research only. **Pitfalls.** One small cross-sectional study, no reliability data, and lighting and camera angle are uncontrolled at home.

#### Swallowing check

**Measures.** The EAT-10 swallowing questionnaire. No phone microphone method has been validated in ALS. **Devices and APIs.** An in-app questionnaire. **Access.** None. **Sampling and region.** Active, about a minute, weekly or monthly; speech and swallowing. **Noise or MDC.** No test-retest or MDC found in ALS.

**ALS evidence (moderate).** Against videofluoroscopy in 70 people, a score of 3 or more detected penetration or aspiration (sensitivity 88%, specificity 57%), and 8 or more detected aspiration (85.7%, 71.9%; negative predictive value 95.5%) ([Plowman 2015](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4688134/)). In 68 people the AUC was 0.873 for unsafe swallowing (abstract only) ([Xia 2023](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37368071/)). Against endoscopy in 113 people, the AUC was 0.790, and a cut-off of 5 gave sensitivity 84.6% ([DOSS 2026](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13149588/)). All were given in clinic, on paper where stated; app delivery is unvalidated. A stethoscope on the sternum separated swallow sounds in 24 people with ALS from much younger controls, but no phone was tested ([Front Neurol 2023, swallow sounds](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10435850/)). **Feeds the tool.** Not a home-test decline. A score of 3 or more (or 8 or more) prompts the clinician to ask about and record `dysphagia` (Mei weight 4.57) and to refer for a swallow assessment. **Pitfalls.** Self-report misses early change; people with ALS do not notice their earliest bulbar changes ([Allison 2017](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5530595/)).

#### Cough strength

**Measures.** Cough peak flow estimated from the sound of a cough. **Devices and APIs.** Phone microphone; a peak flow meter is the reference. **Access.** A research method. **Sampling and region.** Active; speech and swallowing. **Noise or MDC.** No test-retest data; an app estimate agreed with a flow meter at ICC 0.774.

**ALS evidence (moderate).** In 50 neuromuscular patients (26 with ALS), that app estimate had sensitivity 94.4% and specificity 100% for peak flow under 270 L/min, in clinic (abstract only) ([Recasens 2023](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37860934/)). In 60 people with ALS and 40 controls, phone cough sounds separated ALS from controls (AUC 0.85) and were linked to the ALSFRS-R total, but not to measured cough peak flow, and bulbar classification was near chance (AUC 0.51 to 0.63) ([Lisbon 2024](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11649145/)). **Feeds the tool.** Research only. **Pitfalls.** The two studies conflict. Daily home cough-flow checks reached only 37% compliance (abstract only) ([Paneroni 2014](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24491372/)).

### Breathing: strong clinic evidence, weak consumer devices

#### Overnight SpO2

**Measures.** Blood oxygen during sleep, summarised as time below 90% (T90) or dips per hour (ODI). **Devices and APIs.** Apple Watch Series 6 or later or Ultra (HealthKit `oxygenSaturation`). US models bought from 18 Jan 2024 (part numbers ending LW/A or LS/A) compute readings on the iPhone ([Apple Support: Blood Oxygen](https://support.apple.com/en-us/120358)) and are left out of the Vitals app ([Apple Support: Vitals](https://support.apple.com/en-us/120142)). Samsung writes SpO2 to Health Connect. Fitbit and Pixel Watch readings come through the Google Health API at 1-minute resolution, with gaps by design. Other sources are Withings ScanWatch and Garmin Pulse Ox (API only). **Access.** Per platform; in Health Connect, only Samsung wrote SpO2 among the brands checked. **Sampling and region.** Passive, if the watch is worn in bed; breathing.

**Noise or MDC.** With a clinic wrist oximeter in 100 people with MND, night-to-night ICC was 0.940 for T90 and 0.901 for ODI, and one night alone missed 12% of people who qualified for NIV ([Luu 2025, Respirology](https://doi.org/10.1111/resp.70072)). From those ICCs and the night-1 SDs (21.7 points of T90, 10.9 events an hour of ODI), MDC95 is about 14.7 points of T90 and 9.5 events an hour of ODI (computed by us; crude, because T90 is very skewed). Consumer accuracy is worse. A Galaxy Watch 4 had RMSE 2.54 points against a sleep lab ([Browne 2024](https://doi.org/10.5664/jcsm.11178)). Below 88% saturation, an Apple Watch Series 7 had an error of 5.82% ([Jiang 2026](https://doi.org/10.2196/85253)). The wrist lost 30.4% of night data ([Adam 2025](https://doi.org/10.1109/EMBC58623.2025.11252834)), and a Fitbit Sense 2 in hospital had ICC 0.47 with 41% complete data ([Lee 2026](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ijmedinf.2026.106704)) (all abstract only).

**ALS evidence (weak for consumer devices; strong with clinic oximeters).** In 476 clinic patients, overnight oximetry turned abnormal a median 1.6 years after onset, against 3.8 years for FVC below 50% of predicted (1.5 years for MIP). Where a test turned abnormal before symptoms, oximetry did so in 79% (abstract only) ([Kelly 2022](https://doi.org/10.1212/WNL.0000000000200758)). In 69 people, mean night SpO2 below 93% (printed as 93 mmHg in the abstract) went with mean survival of 7.5 against 18.5 months (abstract only) ([Velasco 2002](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12072825/)). Starting NIV at more than 15 dips an hour went with longer survival (abstract only) ([Pinto 2003](https://doi.org/10.1080/14660820310006706)). No consumer SpO2 device has been tested in ALS. **Feeds the tool.** Context only. A sustained 3-night pattern (T90 above about 5% to 10%, or ODI above 15 an hour) prompts a clinic overnight oximetry, and after diagnosis an NIV review. Never lower a risk because SpO2 looks normal. **Pitfalls.** Pulse oximeters over-read in darker skin: occult low oxygen was nearly 3 times as common in Black as in White patients ([Sjoding 2020](https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMc2029240)). Oximetry also missed 31 of 100 people with night hypoventilation (abstract only) ([Boentert 2018](https://doi.org/10.1136/jnnp-2017-316515)).

#### Nightly respiratory rate

**Measures.** Average breaths per minute during sleep. **Devices and APIs.** Apple Watch (`respiratoryRate`, method not stated) ([Apple: respiratoryRate](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/healthkit/hkquantitytypeidentifier/respiratoryrate)); Google Health API `daily-respiratory-rate` for Fitbit and Pixel Watch; Withings Sleep `rr`; Garmin; Oura and WHOOP may offer it through their own APIs (not confirmed). **Access.** Per platform; in Health Connect, only the Google Health app writes it among the brands checked. **Sampling and region.** Passive, nightly; breathing.

**Noise or MDC.** Fitbit's mean 14-day coefficient of variation rose from about 4.7% to 5.0% at ages 20 to 25 to 6.1% to 7.4% at 65 to 69; its 90% range was 2.3% to 9.2% in young women and up to 21.7% in older men. At 15.4 breaths a minute, that gives an MDC95 of about 2.1 (at 5%) to 3.9 (at 9.2%) breaths a minute (computed by us) ([Natarajan 2021](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41746-021-00493-6)). A Galaxy Watch had RMSE 1.13 breaths a minute overnight, with accuracy falling in severe sleep apnea ([Jung 2023](https://doi.org/10.3390/s23187976)) (Jung abstract only).

**ALS evidence (weak).** No ALS study used wearable breathing rate. Indirectly, neuromuscular patients without breathing complaints breathe fast and shallow, and their CO2 rises with rate (abstract only) ([Misuri 2000](https://doi.org/10.1378/chest.117.2.447)). On home NIV, breathing rate in ALS rose more when tidal volume was low (abstract only) ([Orr 2023](https://doi.org/10.1183/23120541.00163-2023)). NICE-based guidance lists a raised breathing rate as a sign of respiratory impairment ([MND Association P6](https://www.mndassociation.org/sites/default/files/public/2022-11/P6%20Respiratory%20symptoms.pdf)). **Feeds the tool.** Context only. A rise of more than about 2 to 4 breaths a minute above the person's own baseline, lasting weeks, prompts a check. **Pitfalls.** Infection: 36.4% of Fitbit users with COVID-19 symptoms had a night at least 3 breaths a minute above usual ([Natarajan 2021](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41746-021-00493-6)). Weight and age also move it. After NIV starts, ventilator logs are better.

#### Breathing disturbances

**Measures.** A nightly count of breathing disturbances and sleep apnea alerts. **Devices and APIs.** Apple Watch Series 9 or later, Ultra 2 or later, or SE 3 ([Apple Support: sleep apnea notifications](https://support.apple.com/en-us/120031)), which uses the accelerometer and needs at least 10 nights in 30 days ([Apple sleep apnea white paper](https://www.apple.com/health/pdf/sleep-apnea/Sleep_Apnea_Notifications_on_Apple_Watch_September_2024.pdf)). Withings Sleep under-mattress pad: `breathing_disturbances_intensity` everywhere, a medical-grade AHI only on European and Australian devices, and the FDA-cleared Withings index in the US (Sleep Rx, on prescription) ([Withings OpenAPI spec](https://developer.withings.com/openapi.yaml); [FDA K231667](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/cdrh_docs/pdf23/K231667.pdf)). **Access.** HealthKit read; Withings `user.sleepevents`, though the plans page lists the cleared AHI only on the paid Advanced Biomarkers plan ([Withings plans](https://static.withings.com/content/api/plans/plans.html)). **Sampling and region.** Passive, nightly; breathing.

**Noise or MDC.** No MDC. Against a home sleep test, Withings AHIs averaged over 1, 3 or 7 nights were not equivalent within 2.5 events an hour (abstract only) ([Jagielski 2022](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11325-022-02751-7)). Accuracy, outside ALS: Apple's feature had a weighted sensitivity of 66.3% and specificity of 98.5% (43.4% for moderate and 89.1% for severe apnea) ([Apple sleep apnea white paper](https://www.apple.com/health/pdf/sleep-apnea/Sleep_Apnea_Notifications_on_Apple_Watch_September_2024.pdf)). The Withings pad had 88.0% and 88.6% at AHI 15 or more in a Withings-run study ([Edouard 2021](https://doi.org/10.5664/jcsm.9168)), with lower figures in independent studies ([Lechat 2026](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41746-026-02914-w)) (both abstract only).

**ALS evidence (weak).** In a sleep lab, 45.6% of 250 non-ventilated people with ALS had an AHI above 5, and 40% had hypoventilation (abstract only) ([Boentert 2018](https://doi.org/10.1136/jnnp-2017-316515)). In mild ALS, a higher AHI predicted new breathlessness within 12 months (abstract only) ([Li 2026, mild ALS](https://doi.org/10.1017/cjn.2026.10557)). But AHI did not track vital capacity, while minimum SpO2 did (abstract only) ([Li 2026, AHI and SpO2](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10072-026-08984-8)). No consumer device has been tested in ALS. **Feeds the tool.** Context only; prompts a sleep study, and after diagnosis a question about breathlessness lying flat. **Pitfalls.** Apple's feature excludes people already diagnosed with sleep apnea and is not for managing it ([FDA K240929](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/cdrh_docs/pdf24/K240929.pdf)). These devices learned the repeated dips of obstructive apnea, not the slow REM-sleep hypoventilation of ALS.

#### Home spirometry

**Measures.** Vital capacity blown into a handheld spirometer at home. **Devices and APIs.** A home spirometer, some with a phone connection; no consumer platform stores lung function ([Health Connect data types](https://developer.android.com/health-and-fitness/guides/health-connect/plan/data-types)). **Access.** A clinic-supplied device, ideally with one coached session. **Sampling and region.** Active, every 1 to 4 weeks; breathing.

**Noise or MDC.** No home test-retest MDC was found. Unsupervised home vital capacity agreed with supervised tests within -15.1 to +15.4% of predicted at baseline, and 85% to 88% of tests were within 10% ([Helleman 2022, home spirometry](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00415-022-10996-1)). Slope estimates settled after about 3 months of data ([Peller 2025](https://doi.org/10.3389/fneur.2025.1588992)).

**ALS evidence (strong).** In 233 trial participants, coached home vital capacity matched clinic values (Lin's r 0.82) and fell 1.882% of predicted a month, against 1.476 in clinic (abstract only) ([Macklin 2026](https://doi.org/10.1002/mus.70322)). In a remote study of 67 people, the 34 whose FVC slopes could be grouped fell into slow (about 0 to -0.4), moderate (-0.95) and fast (-2.09% of predicted a month) groups ([Peller 2025](https://doi.org/10.3389/fneur.2025.1588992)). Breathing comes first in only about 3% of people (abstract only) ([Shoesmith 2007](https://doi.org/10.1136/jnnp.2006.103564)). **Feeds the tool.** A monitoring view after diagnosis, not the referral engine. NICE-based guidance, via an MND Association sheet, says to discuss NIV at a vital capacity below 50%, or below 80% with symptoms ([MND Association P6](https://www.mndassociation.org/sites/default/files/public/2022-11/P6%20Respiratory%20symptoms.pdf)). **Pitfalls.** Weak lips and face make the test harder; it depends on effort. Alex's real clinic FVC on 22 Sep 2026 was 76% of predicted sitting and 61% lying flat.

#### Phone lung-function estimate

**Measures.** FVC predicted from a sustained "ah" by a machine-learning model; maximum phonation time (how long one "ah" lasts); and percent pause while reading, as a breath proxy. **Devices and APIs.** Phone microphone; the FVC model is Aural Analytics'. **Access.** Maximum phonation time and percent pause are simple to build; the FVC model is proprietary. **Sampling and region.** Active; breathing.

**Noise or MDC.** The FVC model had repeatability ICC 0.92 to 0.94 (spirometry 0.97) and a mean absolute error of 0.54 to 0.58 L, or 18.5% to 19.5% (abstract only) ([Stegmann 2021](https://doi.org/10.1080/21678421.2020.1866013)). No phone test-retest data exist for maximum phonation time.

**ALS evidence (moderate).** Predicted FVC correlated 0.80 with measured FVC and picked up decline, though less than spirometry ([Stegmann 2021](https://doi.org/10.1080/21678421.2020.1866013)). In 62 people tested in clinic every 3 months, maximum phonation time predicted impaired cough peak flow (AUC 0.88) and reduced FVC (0.78) ([MPT 2023](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10187964/)). Percent pause in a home phone reading flagged slow vital capacity below 50% with AUC 0.88 in 36 people (abstract only) ([Connaghan 2025](https://doi.org/10.1002/mus.28435)). No ALS study of phone-microphone spirometry was found. **Feeds the tool.** Context only, as a soft trend. **Pitfalls.** An error of about 19% is too coarse for NIV decisions.

## Platform access: the Health export and three free APIs work tonight

### Apple: rich data on the phone, no cloud, research-only extras

A native iOS app needs the HealthKit capability, usage strings and a read permission per data type. It can never tell "denied" from "no data" ([Apple: authorizing access](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/healthkit/authorizing-access-to-health-data)). From iOS 27, a second screen lets the user grant only a recent window of history, and `getEarliestAuthorizedSampleDate(for:)` reports where it starts ([Apple: getEarliestAuthorizedSampleDate](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/healthkit/hkhealthstore/getearliestauthorizedsampledate(for:completion:))). Background delivery needs its own entitlement, and some types are capped at hourly updates: step count on iPhone, and most types on Apple Watch ([Apple: enableBackgroundDelivery](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/healthkit/hkhealthstore/enablebackgrounddelivery(for:frequency:withcompletion:))). The store is locked 10 minutes after the device locks ([Apple Platform Security: health data](https://support.apple.com/guide/security/protecting-access-to-users-health-data-sec88be9900f/web)), and there is no web or cloud API: the device stores all HealthKit data locally ([Apple: protecting user privacy](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/healthkit/protecting-user-privacy)).

The no-app route is the Health app's "Export All Health Data", which exports the data in XML format ([Apple Support: share your data in Health](https://support.apple.com/en-ca/guide/iphone/iph5ede58c3d/ios)). The file is a zip, records are tagged with their HealthKit type, and workout routes come as GPX files, according to an open-source parser ([healthkit-to-sqlite](https://github.com/dogsheep/healthkit-to-sqlite)). The mobility metrics are included ([Sci Rep 2023](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10067003/)). The Health app's own export is probably not limited by the iOS 27 history window, but that was not verified. A paid exporter, Health Auto Export, can post CSV or JSON to a web endpoint (Basic USD 2.99 once; Premium USD 1.99 a month, 6.99 a year or 24.99 for life) ([App Store: Health Auto Export](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/health-auto-export-json-csv/id1115567069)).

SensorKit is the only Apple route to passive keyboard and speech metrics. It needs a private entitlement for an Apple-approved research study, ethics-board approval before distribution, and consent for each sensor ([Apple Health Research: accessing SensorKit data](https://healthresearch.apple.com/resources/accessing-sensorkit-data)). New data are held for 24 hours ([Apple: SRFetchRequest](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/sensorkit/srfetchrequest)), and only 7 days of earlier data are available ([Apple: SRSensorReader](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/sensorkit/srsensorreader), a class that iOS 27 deprecates). In one study, opt-in was 67.0% among White participants and 51.6% among under-represented groups ([Intern Health Study 2025](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0329707)). `CMMovementDisorderManager` measures Parkinsonian tremor and dyskinesia, needs an entitlement, and is only for patients clinically diagnosed with a movement disorder ([Apple: CMMovementDisorderManager](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/coremotion/cmmovementdisordermanager)). ResearchKit is open source, with release 3.4.2 on 23 Sep 2026 ([ResearchKit releases](https://github.com/ResearchKit/ResearchKit/releases)). Its ready-made active tasks include two-finger tapping, a hole peg test, a timed walk, a six-minute walk and voice tasks, and its task header mentions no special entitlement ([ResearchKit task header](https://github.com/ResearchKit/ResearchKit/blob/main/ResearchKitActiveTask/Common/ORKOrderedTask%2BORKPredefinedActiveTask.h)). App Review rule 5.1.3 requires consent and ethics-board approval for health research, and rule 1.4.1 puts medical apps under more scrutiny ([Apple App Review Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)). An Apple Watch needs an iPhone, and watchOS 27 needs an iPhone 11 or later, or SE (2nd generation) or later ([Apple Support: update your Apple Watch](https://support.apple.com/en-us/108926)). Custom Apple Watch apps need a Mac; see the other session's report. In Canada the original Blood Oxygen feature still works; US models bought from 18 Jan 2024 (part numbers ending LW/A or LS/A) compute it on the iPhone ([Apple Newsroom, Aug 2025](https://www.apple.com/newsroom/2025/08/an-update-on-blood-oxygen-for-apple-watch-in-the-us/); [Apple Support: Blood Oxygen](https://support.apple.com/en-us/120358)).

### Android Health Connect: one app reads many brands, but only on the phone

Health Connect is an on-device store, and Google does not keep its data in the cloud ([Google: learn about Health Connect](https://support.google.com/android/answer/13770320?hl=en)). A web app therefore needs a small Android companion that reads and uploads. It is built in from Android 14; on Android 13 or lower it is an app from Google Play, which needs Android 9 or higher ([Health Connect: get started](https://developer.android.com/health-and-fitness/guides/health-connect/develop/get-started)). Each data type needs its own runtime permission, and the manifest must link a privacy policy. Reads reach back only 30 days before the first grant unless the app holds `READ_HEALTH_DATA_HISTORY`, and background reads need `READ_HEALTH_DATA_IN_BACKGROUND` ([Health Connect: read data](https://developer.android.com/health-and-fitness/guides/health-connect/develop/read-data)). Publishing on Google Play needs a Health apps declaration that justifies each data type ([Health Connect: declare access](https://developer.android.com/health-and-fitness/guides/health-connect/publish/declare-access)). Rate-limit numbers are not published. A debug build from Android Studio probably works without a Play declaration, but no page says so and it was not tested. Steps, heart rate, sleep and weight arrive from the Google Health app, Samsung, Garmin and Oura. Respiratory rate came only from the Google Health app and SpO2 only from Samsung, among the brands checked ([Google Health Help: Health Connect](https://support.google.com/fitbit/answer/14506680?hl=en); [Samsung developer blog](https://developer.samsung.com/health/blog/en/accessing-samsung-health-data-through-health-connect)). Garmin shares one way, on Android 14 or later, and leaves out respiration, Pulse Ox and HRV ([Notebookcheck, Garmin and Health Connect](https://www.notebookcheck.net/Garmin-officially-reveals-Google-Health-Connect-support-for-wearables.1054577.0.html)). Oura's export leaves out SpO2 and respiratory rate ([Oura support: Health Connect](https://support.ouraring.com/hc/en-us/articles/10786105824531-Health-Connect-by-Android-Integration)). Withings' list could not be confirmed, because its help page returned HTTP 403.

### Fitbit and the Google Health API: the old API is off, the new one caps unverified apps at 100 users

Support for the legacy Fitbit Web API ended on 30 Sep 2026. Google's overview gives 2 Nov 2026 as the shutdown date ([Google Health API overview](https://developers.google.com/health)), while Fitbit's own pages gave 30 Oct 2026 ([Fitbit Web API intraday page](https://dev.fitbit.com/build/reference/web-api/intraday/)). Both dates have passed, and a probe on 8 Nov 2026 at 04:19 UTC got Google-style 401 errors from the old endpoints. The replacement Google Health API (v4, REST and gRPC) offers 44 data types, none of them raw motion. They include 1-second heart rate, 1-minute steps and SpO2, nightly respiratory rate, HRV, sleep, skin temperature, and weight and body fat from Aria scales ([Google Health API data types](https://developers.google.com/health/data-types)). Every user must consent again, because Fitbit tokens do not carry over ([Google Health API migration guide](https://developers.google.com/health/migration)). Most scopes are restricted. An unverified app is capped at 100 users. A public app needs Google verification plus a yearly CASA security assessment costing USD 500 to 4,500 and taking 2 to 6 weeks ([Google Health API app verification](https://developers.google.com/health/app-verification)). The Fitbit app became the Google Health app on 19 May 2026 ([Google Health Help 17068213](https://support.google.com/googlehealth/answer/17068213?hl=en)), and third-party app installs and updates on Fitbit smartwatches end on 28 Nov 2026 ([Google Health Help 14237121](https://support.google.com/googlehealth/answer/14237121?hl=en)). The Google Fit APIs are deprecated ([Google Fit](https://developers.google.com/fit)).

### Garmin: a free personal key in seconds, three tiers since 21 October

Garmin reopened its Connect Developer Program on 21 Oct 2026 with three tiers ([Garmin program overview](https://developer.garmin.com/gc-developer-program/overview/); [DC Rainmaker](https://www.dcrainmaker.com/2026/10/garmin-connect-api-revamps-connect-developer.html)). **Personal** is a free key for your own data, pull only, with no approval; one reviewer created a key in about 8 seconds ([DC Rainmaker](https://www.dcrainmaker.com/2026/10/garmin-connect-api-revamps-connect-developer.html)). **Starter** is a free business tier for up to 1,000 users, push only, with no history. **Enterprise** charges a negotiated fee per active user, with a USD 1,000 entry fee credited toward it, and adds ping/pull delivery and backfill ([DC Rainmaker](https://www.dcrainmaker.com/2026/10/garmin-connect-api-revamps-connect-developer.html)). The Health API covers steps, heart rate, sleep, stress, Pulse Ox, respiration, body composition, blood pressure, Body Battery and epoch summaries; approved developers get an evaluation environment, and the page shows no field-level documents ([Garmin Health API](https://developer.garmin.com/gc-developer-program/enterprise/health-api/)). Runalyze reports that Garmin strips some calculated fields, such as VO2 max, from API files, and Garmin has not said whether a Personal key gets the full file ([the5krunner](https://the5krunner.com/2026/10/22/garmin-personal-api/)). The Health SDK needs an enterprise licence ([Garmin Health SDK](https://developer.garmin.com/health-sdk/overview/)).

### Samsung: on-device only; use Health Connect

The Samsung Health Data SDK reads steps, heart rate, sleep, SpO2, body composition and more from the Samsung Health app. It needs Samsung Health 6.30.2 or later and Android 10 or later, and has no cloud API ([Samsung Health Data SDK](https://developer.samsung.com/health/data/overview.html)). Developer mode allows read-only testing; real users, or writing data, need a Samsung partnership ([Samsung developer mode](https://developer.samsung.com/health/data/guide/developer-mode.html)). The older Samsung Health SDK for Android was deprecated on 31 Jul 2025 ([Samsung SDK release notes](https://developer.samsung.com/health/android/release-note.html)) and keeps working for at least two years after that ([Samsung migration guide](https://developer.samsung.com/health/data/migration-guide/overview.html)). The watch-side Sensor SDK gives raw accelerometer, PPG, ECG and BIA on Galaxy Watch4 and later, and distribution needs a partnership ([Samsung Health Sensor SDK](https://developer.samsung.com/health/sensor/overview.html)). The simple route is Health Connect: Samsung writes steps, SpO2, sleep, and weight with body fat, but not respiratory rate or HRV ([Samsung developer blog](https://developer.samsung.com/health/blog/en/accessing-samsung-health-data-through-health-connect)).

### Withings: the best cloud API for weight and night breathing, with a new paywall

The Public API uses OAuth 2.0 and webhooks. Its free plan covers up to 10 users, with more after a short review. Apps should poll no more than once every 10 minutes per user ([Withings plans](https://static.withings.com/content/api/plans/plans.html); [Withings API reference](https://developer.withings.com/llms.md)). Since 12 Oct 2026, new accounts need a Withings+ membership to share data with free-plan apps, and also to share through Apple Health or Google; older accounts are unaffected ([Withings plans](https://static.withings.com/content/api/plans/plans.html)). The store prices Withings+ at USD 99.50 or CAD 129.99 a year and says "Your data stays free" ([Withings Sleep, US store](https://www.withings.com/us/en/sleep-analyzer); [Withings Sleep, Canada store](https://www.withings.com/ca/en/sleep-analyzer)). Scales give weight, fat-free and muscle mass and segmental values. Sleep pads give breathing rate, breathing-disturbance intensity and, depending on region, an AHI or the FDA-cleared Withings index. ScanWatch gives SpO2 and respiratory rate ([Withings: all available health data](https://developer.withings.com/developer-guide/v3/data-api/all-available-health-data/); [Withings OpenAPI spec](https://developer.withings.com/openapi.yaml)). The plans page lists cleared biomarkers, including SpO2 and the AHI, only on the paid Advanced Biomarkers plan, so a free-plan app may not get them. Whether the AHI or Sleep Rx is available in Canada was not found.

### The realistic path for this team tonight

1. Keep the engine's four home tests. Feed Alex's simulated series in real API field names (HealthKit `bodyMass` or Withings `getmeas` type 1 for weight), labelled simulated on screen.
2. Add at most one live connector, for a teammate's own data, labelled "teammate, real". An iPhone owner can export Health data and parse `Record` elements for `walkingSpeed`, `stepCount` and `bodyMass` into weekly medians: no app, no approval, no Mac. A Garmin owner can use a Personal key. A Withings owner with an account made before 12 Oct 2026 can use OAuth and poll. Withings' developer FAQ has said localhost callback URLs are not allowed, so plan on a public HTTPS callback, such as a tunnel ([Withings developer FAQ, 2021 copy](https://web.archive.org/web/20211128225339/https://developer.withings.com/developer-guide/faq/faq/)). A Fitbit or Pixel Watch owner can use the Google Health API as an unverified app through Google's OAuth Playground codelab ([Google Health API: get started](https://developers.google.com/health/get-started)).
3. Stretch goal: a Kotlin Health Connect reader on a teammate's Android phone. Check in the first hour that a debug build can get permissions.
4. Skip SensorKit, `CMMovementDisorderManager`, the Samsung SDKs, the Garmin Health SDK, the Fitbit OS SDK, Google Fit, Google verification, and any custom watch app (an Apple Watch app needs a Mac).
5. Say on the honesty slide that none of these signals is validated for detecting undiagnosed ALS, and that home tests run alongside referral, never instead of it.

**The notes' false-alarm simulations.** The researchers simulated a stable person: normal noise, independent weeks, no learning, a baseline followed by 8 weekly tests, and the two-in-a-row rule (computed by us). For tapping at 6 taps, false alarms were 33% (noise SEM 6.4 taps, single-reading baseline), 26% (baseline the mean of 3), 42% (SEM 9.05, single) and 39% (SEM 9.05, mean of 3). At an MDC95 threshold they fell to about 2% (single) and 0.2% (mean of 3). For reading at 15 wpm with controls-level noise (SEM 11.3 wpm), the chance of a false flag over 8 weeks was about 22%. It was about 9% at 22.1 wpm, about 2% at 31.3 wpm, and about 3% at 22.1 wpm with a 3-reading baseline. With ALS-level noise (SEM 8.0 wpm), 15 wpm gave about 10% (single) and 3.5% (3-reading).

**A check of the engine's exact rule (computed by us for this report).** The engine's baseline is the mean of the first two results, which neither simulation used. We reran the same model with the engine's rule: 8 weekly tests in all, baseline the mean of tests 1 and 2, and a flag on two consecutive later tests each beyond the MDC. Our code reproduced the notes' figures under their setup (32.8% against their 33% for 6 taps), which checks the method. The notes' 26% to 42% for 6 taps is higher than the engine's because their stable person had a 1- or 3-reading baseline and then 8 more tests, so 7 chances for two bad weeks in a row; the engine's 8 tests include its 2-test baseline, which leaves 5. Tapping SEMs come from WatchALS's dominant-hand MDC95 of 35.5 taps per 20 s, halved for a 10-s test (6.4) or divided by √2 (9.05). Gait-speed SEMs are WatchALS's MDC95 divided by 2.772, and weight's SEM is 0.85 kg from Bhutani 2017.

For the non-dominant hand, WatchALS's MDC95 of 32.6 gives an SEM of 5.9 or 8.3 taps: 20.7% or 31.0% false alarms at 6 taps, and 0.1% or 2.1% at 18 taps (computed by us).

In an unclear case any single decline escalates, so false alarms add up across tests. With today's placeholders, about 44% to 62% of stable people would get at least one false decline across both hands, reading and weight within 8 weeks (both hands given the dominant hand's noise; 42% to 60% with each hand's own). With the recommended MDCs, including gait speed, it would be about 2% to 7% (computed by us, assuming the tests are independent). All of these figures assume independent weekly errors. Real signals drift together for weeks (holidays, illness, season), so real false-alarm rates will be higher, especially for weight.

**Two practice weeks before the baseline.** Scores rise with repetition, so a baseline taken in weeks 1 and 2 sits low and hides real decline. In WatchALS, both groups improved on hand and speech tasks over 24 weeks ([WatchALS](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13423962/)). Remote readers in the control group gained 0.25 wpm a week ([Kothare 2023](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11246071/)). Stegmann's team skipped the first two weeks "to avoid task familiarization" ([Stegmann 2024](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11496019/)), and syllable-repetition counts rose during a trial run-in ([Neumann 2026](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13324186/)). On a phone pinching task, half the practice gain took 28 repetitions, while a 2-minute walk showed no practice effect (MS, abstract only) ([Woelfle 2021](https://doi.org/10.2196/30394)). Repeat 2- and 6-minute walks still gained 4.3 m and 11.2 m (abstract only) ([Knak 2017](https://doi.org/10.2340/16501977-2222)). Two practice weeks reduce this problem but do not remove it. They move the earliest possible flag from the 4th weekly test to the 6th, which still fits in 8 weeks. In Alex's simulated tapping, dropping two practice weeks also removes the placeholder's flag (checked by running the engine).

**Team calibration measures the wrong noise.** The app's calibration takes 10 trials in a row per teammate and computes MDC95 = 1.96 × √2 × pooled within-person SD. Same-session repeats leave out day-to-day and week-to-week variation and mix in practice and fatigue within the session, so the result will probably come out smaller than weekly noise. Treat it as a check, and use the larger of the team's figure and the published one. To measure weekly noise, test 10 to 15 people on 2 or 3 sessions about a week apart. Use the within-person SD as the SEM, compute MDC95 = 1.96 × √2 × SEM, and report it per hand ([Bohannon 2021](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8332647/); [Boukhvalova 2018](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6131483/)).

**Other engine changes we recommend.**
1. `unclear_with_decline` fires on any home decline, including weight (region general). Its stated reason is "progressive motor impairment", which weight loss is not. Restrict it to arm, leg, bulbar and breathing declines, or keep weight on Route 2 only. The rule tests the number after the decline's own odds ratio is added, so a decline can also lift a case that was low on its notes into "unclear" and then fire it (checked by running the engine).
2. `detectDecline`'s comment says "the two most recent results", but the code returns the first qualifying pair anywhere in the series, so a flag never clears. Decide which is intended. For passive history, feed only a recent window.
3. `home_decline` enters the number once however many tests decline. At the 10% line, Alex's simulated tapping decline alone made the tier "high" on 2 Dec 2024 by number, with no rule firing (checked).
4. Use controls-level MDCs before diagnosis and ALS-level MDCs after it.
5. Word the 8-week step-down as "no decline seen; this does not rule out ALS", and keep the safety-net advice.

## Pitfalls: walking aids, carrying, updates and holidays move signals more than 8 weeks of ALS

### Confounders, with sizes

**Walking aids and orthoses.** Wrist trackers had "close to 100%" step error in older adults using rollators ([Copenhagen rollator study 2020](https://doi.org/10.1186/s11556-019-0235-0)). Phone gait accuracy with walking aids fell to correlations as low as 0.35 and ICC 0.22 ([Sensors 2024, walking aids](https://doi.org/10.3390/s24175839)). People with ALS who "walk with assistance" took 1283 steps a day, against 3984 for normal walkers ([Straczkiewicz 2025](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-025-24664-7)). Gait speed fell 0.135 m/s a month just before a switch to a walking aid ([Lukac](https://doi.org/10.1159/000530067)). Evidence on ankle-foot orthoses is uncertain. A meta-analysis found no significant effect on the 10-m walk (very low certainty) ([Mijic 2024](https://doi.org/10.1177/22143602241289216)), while a custom orthosis raised stride length and speed in 10 people with foot drop ([Caravaggi 2024](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.gaitpost.2024.01.017)) (both abstract only). Log every new aid or brace and restart the baseline.

**Where the phone is carried.** In daily life, iPhone step counts were 21.5% (1,340 steps a day) off a worn accelerometer, probably because the phone was not always carried (abstract only) ([Duncan 2018](https://doi.org/10.1080/02640414.2017.1409855)). In ALS, the phone moved with the person for only about 191 minutes a day ([Karas 2024](https://doi.org/10.1002/acn3.52050)). One participant's partner often carried her phone ([Johnson 2023](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41746-023-00778-y)). iPhone gait metrics need the phone in a pocket near the hip ([Apple mobility white paper](https://www.apple.com/healthcare/docs/site/Measuring_Walking_Quality_Through_iPhone_Mobility_Metrics.pdf)).

**Software and firmware updates.** Oura's new step algorithm cut counts by 20% on average ([TechRadar 2025](https://www.techradar.com/health-fitness/smartwatches/activity-tracking-on-oura-ring-is-about-to-get-a-whole-lot-better-but-ive-got-bad-news-about-your-step-count)). A March 2026 Pixel Watch update doubled or tripled some users' daily steps, and old data were left unchanged ([Android Central 2026](https://www.androidcentral.com/wearables/google-pixel-watch/dont-trust-your-pixel-watch-steps-after-the-latest-firmware-update)). An iOS app update broke syncing in the middle of a study ([Copenhagen rollator study 2020](https://doi.org/10.1186/s11556-019-0235-0)). One ALS study compared only sessions recorded with the same firmware ([Gupta 2023](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10442344/)). Log device, firmware and app version with every reading, and restart the baseline after a change.

**Season.** Summer brought 394.5 more daily steps than winter in 543 Fitbit users in a depression study (abstract only) ([Zhang 2024](https://doi.org/10.2196/55302)). In 120 Swedish adults with prediabetes or type 2 diabetes, January averaged 6,944 steps against 7,993 in May, about 13% lower, and winter 7,098 against 7,825 in summer ([Sophia Step Study 2026](https://doi.org/10.3390/ijerph23010053)). In UK Biobank wrist data, by contrast, season effects were small (d = 0.27 in men, 0.15 in women; abstract only) ([Doherty 2017](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0169649)). The sources disagree on size. An 8-week window starting now runs into December either way, so compare with a season-matched baseline or require a drop larger than the seasonal swing.

**Holidays.** Within 10 days after Christmas, Withings users' weight rose 0.4% (US), 0.6% (Germany) and 0.5% (Japan) ([Helander 2016](https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMc1602012)). In another cohort, Christmas added 1.35% (about 1.10 kg), and 0.35% was still there in March ([Turicchi 2020](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0232152)). Steps were lowest on Christmas and New Year's Eve ([Sophia Step Study 2026](https://doi.org/10.3390/ijerph23010053)). Helander 2016 and the weekly-weight study by Orsama 2014 both list Brian Wansink as a co-author; PubMed listed neither as retracted on 8 Nov 2026, but do not rely on them alone ([Orsama 2014](https://doi.org/10.1159/000356147)).

**Practice effects.** On repeated phone tests in MS, Finger Pinching improved by up to 86.2% and Draw a Shape by 23.1%, with no practice effect on a 2-minute walk (abstract only) ([Woelfle 2021](https://doi.org/10.2196/30394)). In WatchALS, both groups improved on hand and speech tasks over 24 weeks ([WatchALS](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13423962/)).

**Carpal tunnel and other look-alikes.** Carpal tunnel syndrome lowered all four tapping measures in 52 patients against 45 controls ([Seok 2019](https://doi.org/10.1002/mus.26424)). In a survey, 11% of hand surgeons had operated on nerves in patients later diagnosed with ALS, and failure to improve after surgery is a red flag ([Thomson 2023](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jhsa.2023.03.023)). In 13 of 554 people with ALS, nerve compression had been confused with early ALS ([Kollewe 2011](https://doi.org/10.3109/17482968.2011.572980)) (all abstract only). Alex had exactly this: a carpal tunnel release that did not help.

**Injury.** After lower-limb fractures, daily steps in Apple Health fell 93% and were still 52% below baseline at 6 months (abstract only) ([JBJS 2025, steps](https://doi.org/10.2106/JBJS.24.00842)).

**Age.** Gait speed falls about 0.03 m/s a year with age (abstract only) ([Ip 2022](https://doi.org/10.1093/gerona/glac109)), about 0.005 m/s in 8 weeks (computed by us). That is negligible against a person's own baseline but matters against norms. Wrist activity is 7.5% lower per decade (abstract only) ([Doherty 2017](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0169649)). At 45, Alex is younger than most ALS cohorts.

**Oximeter bias with darker skin.** When pulse oximeters read 92% to 96%, true saturation was below 88% in 11.7% of Black and 3.6% of White patients at one hospital, and in 17.0% and 6.2% across 178 hospitals ([Sjoding 2020](https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMc2029240)). A meta-analysis found oximeters over-read by 1.27% in dark skin against 0.70% in light skin (abstract only) ([Singh 2024, oximetry](https://doi.org/10.2196/62769)). The FDA's January 2025 draft guidance does not cover wellness devices ([FDA draft guidance](https://www.fda.gov/media/184896/download)).

**Time of day.** In 10 older inpatients, weight swung 1.1% to 3.6% over 9 hours (abstract only) ([Vivanti 2013](https://doi.org/10.1111/jhn.12034)). Alex's speaking rate was 165 wpm at 9 am and 118 wpm at 4 pm.

### Adherence: 80% to 95% with support, much lower without it

With staff calls after missed sessions, 85.07% of weekly WatchALS batteries were done ([WatchALS](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13423962/)). In ALS at Home, which asked for daily multi-device measurements for 3 months and then twice a week, 113 people enrolled and only 17 reached 9 months; some feared watching their numbers fall ([Rutkove 2020](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7359124/)). At 3 months, 77%, 59% and 96% of three phone-study cohorts were still doing weekly tasks (abstract only) ([Beukenhorst 2022](https://doi.org/10.2196/31877)). In a Dutch home-monitoring app, 83% of patients kept up weekly weighing, and 4% dropped out (abstract only) ([Helleman 2020](https://doi.org/10.1080/21678421.2020.1718712)). Monthly home tests kept 94% of people for 3 months at 83.2% adherence, and speech was rated the most burdensome task (2.5 out of 10) (abstract only) ([Botman 2026](https://doi.org/10.1080/21678421.2026.2696382)). Wearable data thin out over time. The share of people giving at least 3 days of data fell from 92% to 56% by week 48, and 6 of 25 had skin reactions to a chest patch ([Garcia-Gancedo 2019](https://doi.org/10.2196/13433)). Daily wear fell from 9.1 to 6.5 hours ([Gupta 2023](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10442344/)). Only 21% of patients in German ALS centres used remote ALSFRS-R (abstract only) ([Meyer 2022](https://doi.org/10.1080/21678421.2022.2104649)). Of 332 people with MND surveyed, 69% were happy to self-monitor and 15% would not want home measurements (abstract only) ([Helleman 2022, survey](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00415-022-11273-x)). No study measured adherence before diagnosis.

### Equity: phones reach most people, wearables reach the well-off

In the US, 91% of adults own a smartphone, but only 79% of those 65 and older, 80% of those with household income under USD 30,000, and 88% of rural adults ([Pew Research Center 2026](https://www.pewresearch.org/internet/fact-sheet/mobile/)). In Canada, 84% owned one in 2020, but only 54% of seniors ([Statistics Canada 2023](https://www.statcan.gc.ca/o1/en/plus/3582-so-long-landline-hello-smartphone)). In 2024, 41.1% of US adults used a wearable, but only 45.6% of users wore it daily, so about 19% of US adults use one daily (computed by us). Users skew young, well-off and educated: only 12.5% were 65 or older, 73.3% had household income of USD 50,000 or more, and 81.8% had college education ([Pedroso 2026](https://doi.org/10.1001/jamanetworkopen.2026.17733)). The latest Canadian figure is 24.68% owning a wearable or smart medical device in 2017, from a non-probability panel. Use was less likely with income of CAD 60,000 or less (OR 0.38), and 25.54% of owners had stopped using theirs ([Paré 2018](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5956159)). Willingness to share with clinicians fell to 73.4% in 2024, and only 19.2% actually shared ([Pedroso 2026](https://doi.org/10.1001/jamanetworkopen.2026.17733)).

iOS carries 64.74% of mobile web use in Canada but only 51.96% in the US ([StatCounter Canada](https://gs.statcounter.com/os-market-share/mobile/canada); [StatCounter US](https://gs.statcounter.com/os-market-share/mobile/united-states-of-america)). This is web usage share, not ownership, but an Apple-only design would miss about a third of Canadian phone users and about half of US users. Apple Watch needs an iPhone, while Pixel and newer Samsung watches do not work with iPhones. ALS device cohorts were 85% to 97% White, for example 94.7% in WatchALS ([WatchALS](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13423962/)). In pooled phone studies, Black participants had more accelerometer gaps, and Android users had more GPS gaps than iPhone users ([Karas 2024](https://doi.org/10.1002/acn3.52050)). Speech models were built on American English ([Stegmann 2020](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7555482/)), and VOC-ALS is Italian only ([Dubbioso 2024, VOC-ALS](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11271596/)). Always offer a phone-only path, so that people without a wearable are not left out.

### Privacy, briefly

Once an Ontario clinician collects device data, it becomes personal health information under PHIPA. Consent must be knowledgeable, and disclosure to anyone who is not a custodian needs express consent ([Ontario PHIPA](https://www.ontario.ca/laws/statute/04p03)). Store features, not raw audio or keystrokes. The validated ALS keyboard recorded timing and key zones without recording text ([Acien 2024](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11263713/)).

## Conclusion

The honest pitch is narrow and strong. A phone can run three region-specific tests whose noise is known in people with ALS, and a scale can supply years of weight history. None of it detects ALS before diagnosis, so in an unclear case the tests can only document change that the clinical notes have already flagged. Two engine changes matter more than any new sensor. Sourced MDCs with two practice weeks would cut false declines in stable people from roughly half to a few percent. A region rule would stop a weight-only decline from escalating an unclear case by itself. The next evidence step is cheap: measure week-to-week noise for the app's own tests in 10 to 15 people over 2 to 3 weeks, and check the stopwatch walk against a clinic 10-m walk.
